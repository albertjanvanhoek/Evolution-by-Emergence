# Evolution by Emergence v20 — Theory Core for Peer Review

**Status:** frozen candidate theory for adversarial peer review  
**Scope:** substrate-agnostic cumulative evolution under explicit retention, transition, accessibility, emergence, and resource constraints

## 1. Question

Evolution by Emergence asks:

> **How can organization that exists now become causal material that changes which organization can exist next?**

The v20 theory does not claim that every real system satisfies one universal mechanism. It proposes a substrate-agnostic causal architecture and machine-checks consequences that follow when its interfaces are instantiated.

The candidate cross-domain architecture is:

    history / interaction
      -> retained organization
      -> transition machinery
      -> future accessibility
      -> new organization
      -> budget-constrained retention
      -> ...

This is a theory of **cumulative evolution** because retained products can alter the transition structure through which later products become accessible.

## 2. State and resource accounting

The core state is

    S_t = (G_t, R_t, Gamma_t, B_t^gross)

where G_t is active organization, R_t retained organization, Gamma_t context, and B_t^gross gross available budget.

Retained organization is not free:

    B_t^free = B_t^gross - M(R_t).

This gives retained history two simultaneous effects:

1. **causal leverage:** R_t can alter later transition machinery and accessibility;
2. **maintenance burden:** R_t consumes capacity that could otherwise support maintenance, search, response, or change.

The theory therefore does not model cumulative evolution as unlimited memory.

## 3. Transition machinery induces accessibility

TransitionAccessibility.lean defines a weighted directed transition kernel K, finite routes generated by that kernel, route cost, and finite-horizon reachability under a free budget.

If one kernel preserves every transition of another and never raises an old transition cost, accessibility cannot decrease at equal horizon and free budget:

    K+ dominates K-  =>  A_T^(K-)(B) subseteq A_T^(K+)(B).

Maintenance is then subtracted from the same gross budget.

### Necessary structural condition for paid opening

If the retained arm pays at least as much upkeep as the ablated arm and nevertheless opens a target that is closed in the ablated arm, Lean proves that the ablated kernel cannot reproduce every retained transition at equal or lower cost.

This is a **necessary structural condition**, not an iff characterization.

### Sufficient quantitative condition

If the retained arm has a route of cost C+, every ablated route within the horizon costs at least L-, and

    C+ + (M(R+) - M(R-)) < L-,

then there exists a common gross budget at which the target is accessible with retained organization and inaccessible without it.

This is a **sufficient quantitative condition**.

The generic retained-organization core is bridged to this induced transition semantics: positive paid transfer under kernel-induced accessibility is exactly retained reachability plus ablated non-reachability under each arm's own post-maintenance budget.

## 4. Paid counterfactual transfer

For a retained item X, compare two matched counterfactual arms built from the same active organization, context, and gross budget:

    R+ = Retain(R,X)
    R- = Lose(R,X).

A cumulative paid-transfer event requires, among other declared conditions, positive marginal upkeep, later causal reuse under ablation, budget-monotone accessibility, positive transfer, and a target outside the declared visited set.

The criterion is designed to reject the weak claim that 'the state changed, therefore cumulative evolution occurred.' A retained item must make a difference **after paying for itself**.

## 5. Emergence and the assembly barrier

Strict compositional emergence is treated separately from retention. For a declared whole X, context, and capacity phi:

    X realizes phi
    no proper subconfiguration realizes phi.

If target-specific benefit is unavailable until phi is realized and a proper intermediate has positive upkeep, then that future target function cannot finance the proper intermediate by itself.

Lean proves that a proper, positively costly intermediate of a strictly emergent whole has negative target-financed net value under the declared benefit condition. If such an intermediate nevertheless remains viable, some positive auxiliary support is required.

That support can represent another current function, reuse elsewhere, exaptation, subsidy, drift, or another declared mechanism.

### Transition-mediated specialization

TransitionMediatedEmergence.lean derives the same barrier through transition machinery: if realizing phi is what adds a transition, then a proper non-realizing part gets no phi-mediated kernel advantage. With non-decreasing upkeep, it cannot show positive paid opening through that mechanism.

The module also contains countermodels showing that dropping emergence can let a part transfer; favorable upkeep accounting can create an apparent opening without transition improvement; and a proper part may persist as a stepping stone through another transition.

## 6. Finite resources force retention trade-offs

GenerativeLeverage.lean formalizes bounded reuse. If every accessible target can be injectively assigned to one retained unit and one of at most d support slots, then

    |A| <= |R| d.

With minimum per-unit maintenance mu and total maintenance bounded by B:

    |A| mu <= B d.

The same maintenance ledger yields a direct retention no-go. Let C be a finite candidate retained set. If every candidate costs at least mu > 0 and

    B < |C| mu,

then retaining all of C is budget-infeasible.

Therefore finite resources force a **retention trade-off** when positive-cost candidate history outruns maintenance capacity.

The theorem deliberately does not decide whether a system responds by forgetting, replacement, compression, cheaper representation, greater reuse, compositional support, or increased resource capture.

## 7. The recursive geometry

The theory should be read as a loop, not only as the one-way relation R -> K -> A:

    retained organization R_t
       -> transition machinery K_t
       -> accessibility A_t
       -> generated organization
       -> feasible, budget-constrained retention
       -> R_(t+1)

while simultaneously

    R_t -> maintenance M(R_t) -> free budget -> feasible change.

Each pass can change the structure through which the next pass occurs. The retained past therefore both enables and constrains the future.

## 8. Nested learning-like dynamics

The same abstract geometry may recur at nested organizational layers. Examples worth testing include intracellular and cellular state, organismal and immune organization, neural learning, individual skill and memory, language and cumulative culture, science and institutions, and technological systems.

This is best called **learning-like dynamics**, not a claim that every substrate literally learns in the psychological sense.

The candidate universality claim is relational: history can become retained organization; retained organization can alter future transitions; finite resources constrain what can remain retained.

Higher and lower layers may also change the context Gamma_t encountered by the focal layer. A person can learn and forget while the surrounding technological and social repertoire changes simultaneously.

Lean does **not** prove that all these domains share one empirical mechanism. Cross-domain instantiation is a scientific test of the abstraction.

## 9. Open-endedness and bounded leverage

A finite retained repertoire can support a larger accessible repertoire through reuse. But if retained cardinality and per-unit reuse remain uniformly bounded, accessible cardinality is also bounded under the declared encoding.

Thus open-ended accessibility requires at least one restriction to fail: retained repertoire grows, resource budget grows, per-unit leverage grows, maintenance cost falls, compositional support escapes the bounded single-unit encoding, or the declared representation is incomplete.

This is a constraint on open-ended cumulative evolution, not a theorem that any particular system will become open-ended.

## 10. What the theory claims

The peer-review object claims that the repository provides machine-checked, substrate-agnostic constraints connecting retained organization, maintenance cost, transition machinery, future accessibility, emergence, and bounded generative leverage.

The proposed scientific interpretation is that cumulative evolution can be studied as resource-constrained recursive reorganization in which retained history changes the accessibility structure of later change.

## 11. What the theory does not claim

The formalization does not establish that all systems instantiate the architecture; that the architecture is empirically universal; that all state dependence is cumulative evolution; that retention is always beneficial; that novelty is improvement; that persistence is function; that validation is truth; that selection is progress; that learning-like dynamics imply literal cognition; that emergence guarantees retention; that a moving envelope proves strong unprestatable ontology creation; that machine checking establishes empirical truth; or that a moral, legal, or political conclusion follows from the mathematics.

The current Lean models use fixed ambient types. Strong creation of previously unstatable observables or ontologies is not machine-checked.

## 13. How to challenge it

A useful review should try to break at least one of five things:

1. **Formal validity** — find a theorem whose conclusion does not follow from its exact premises.
2. **Semantic adequacy** — show that a formal predicate does not mean what the prose says it means.
3. **Prior art** — identify an equal or stronger existing architecture.
4. **Explanatory value** — show that the factorization adds no useful distinction.
5. **Cross-domain mapping** — find a genuine cumulative-evolution system that cannot instantiate the interfaces without arbitrary relabeling.

A sixth and especially valuable attack is a **counterexample to universality**: a bona fide cumulative evolutionary process in which retained organization does not become causal structure for later accessibility under any non-arbitrary mapping.

A successful falsification, narrowing, or prior-art correction is a positive scientific result.
