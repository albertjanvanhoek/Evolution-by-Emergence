# Candidate Universal Constraint Architecture

Status: **post-v19 draft research architecture**.

This document is not a priority claim and does not declare a universal law established.

## 1. What is not the new law

Two statements are useful background but are not EbE novelty.

1. **Predictive memory / state augmentation.** A history-dependent stochastic
   process can be represented using a state that summarizes the predictive
   information in its past. Computational mechanics makes this precise through
   causal states and minimal predictive representations (Shalizi & Crutchfield,
   2001).

2. **Memory has physical cost.** In driven physical systems, retained
   nonpredictive information is connected to thermodynamic inefficiency and
   dissipation (Still, Sivak, Bell & Crooks, 2012,
   DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.109.120604).

These results motivate the retained-organization programme but are foundations,
not contributions claimed here.

## 2. Measurement instrument: paid counterfactual transfer

For one retained item X, compare matched retained and ablated arms:

[
R^+ = \operatorname{Retain}(R,X),
\qquad
R^- = \operatorname{Lose}(R,X).
]

Both start from the same active organization, context, and gross budget.

The post-v19 instrument asks whether retaining X changes future accessibility
after its own maintenance burden is paid.

The older PR64 interface allows application-defined graded accessibility.
The stronger theorem layer below derives finite-horizon budget accessibility
from transition machinery itself.

Paid transfer is an operational criterion. It is **not by itself a universal
law**.

## 3. Candidate invariant A: transition machinery induces accessibility

TransitionAccessibility.lean defines a weighted directed transition kernel,
actual finite routes generated by that kernel, route cost, and finite-horizon
accessibility under a free budget.

If K-plus preserves every transition of K-minus and never raises the cost of an
old transition, then every target accessible under K-minus at horizon T and free
budget B remains accessible under K-plus:

[
\boxed{
\mathcal K^+ \preceq \mathcal K^-
\Longrightarrow
\mathcal A_T^{\mathcal K^-}(B)
\subseteq
\mathcal A_T^{\mathcal K^+}(B).
}
]

Maintenance is then paid from one common gross budget.

A regression theorem proves that if retained and ablated organization induce
the same kernel and retention costs weakly more, retention cannot create a paid
opening.

A constructive witness defines the kernels, route, upkeep and budget explicitly
and derives an item-specific positive paid opening.

### Failure modes

The result does not say every retained item improves the kernel. Retention may
remove transitions, raise costs, or impose enough upkeep to eliminate an
otherwise useful transition advantage.

## 4. Candidate invariant B: emergent assembly barrier

EmergentAssemblyBarrier.lean uses strict compositional emergence:

[
X \models \phi,
\qquad
K \not\models \phi
\quad\forall K\prec X.
]

Add an explicit all-or-nothing target-benefit condition: target-specific benefit
from phi is zero before phi is realized.

If every proper intermediate has positive upkeep, then every proper
intermediate has negative net value when required to finance itself from the
future target function phi alone:

[
\boxed{
K\prec X
\land E(X,\phi)
\land M(K)>0
\Longrightarrow
V_\phi(K)-M(K)<0.
}
]

Therefore a gradual retained path to a strictly emergent function cannot be
financed by that future function alone.

If such an intermediate nevertheless remains viable, Lean proves that strictly
positive auxiliary support is required.

That support may be instantiated as another current function, reuse elsewhere,
exaptation, environmental or organizational subsidy, drift / neutral
persistence, or another application-specific mechanism.

### Failure modes

The theorem is not a claim that all emergent functions create an absolute
barrier. It explicitly fails when proper intermediates already obtain partial
target-related benefit, upkeep is zero, or another support channel exists.

## 5. Candidate invariant C: bounded-memory generative leverage

GenerativeLeverage.lean defines bounded single-unit reuse operationally.

Every accessible target must be assigned injectively to one retained unit and
one of at most d support slots for that unit.

Then:

[
\boxed{
|\mathcal A| \le |R|d.
}
]

With minimum per-unit maintenance mu and total maintenance bounded by B, the
theorem becomes:

[
\boxed{
|\mathcal A|\,\mu \le Bd.
}
]

Hence an observed repertoire satisfying

[
|\mathcal A|\,\mu > Bd
]

cannot be explained by the declared bounded single-unit reuse architecture.

At least one restriction must fail: retained repertoire is larger, per-unit
reuse leverage is larger, minimum upkeep is smaller, available maintenance
budget is larger, or accessibility depends on genuinely compositional support
that cannot be assigned to one retained unit/slot pair.

For a time-indexed family, uniformly bounded retained cardinality and uniformly
bounded per-unit reuse rule out unbounded accessible cardinality.

### Failure modes

This is not a claim that all accessible targets have a unique causal owner.
Failure of the encoding is scientifically informative: it can indicate
compositional or synergistic support.

## 6. Why this is compatible with changing phase spaces

The theorem statements are polymorphic over arbitrary types. They do not
enumerate future biological traits, technologies, meanings, or functions.

The universality claim being investigated is therefore relational:

[
\text{retention}
\rightarrow
\text{transition structure}
\rightarrow
\text{accessibility},
]

plus resource and emergence constraints on that relation.

This does not amount to an entailing law of motion over a fixed prestated phase
space.

The present Lean development still uses fixed ambient types inside each model,
so strong creation of previously unstatable observables or ontologies is not
machine-checked.

## 7. What would justify the word "universal"

The same theorem architecture should be instantiated without changing its
logical structure in genuinely different substrates, for example biological
evolution, neural learning, language/cumulative culture, and technological
evolution.

A universality claim would require more than metaphorical mapping. Each domain
must supply measurable counterparts for retained organization, maintenance,
transition machinery, horizon, accessibility, ablation, and reuse.

The strongest next empirical test is whether the same paid-transfer and
generative-leverage quantities can be operationalized across those domains.

## 8. Publication claim at the present stage

A defensible current formulation is:

> We provide machine-checked substrate-agnostic constraints linking retained
> organization, transition-induced future accessibility, maintenance cost,
> strict compositional emergence, and bounded generative leverage.

A stronger formulation such as "a universal law of evolution" should be
reserved for after cross-domain instantiation and adversarial comparison with
neighbouring theories.
