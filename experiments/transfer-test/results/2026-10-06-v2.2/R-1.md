## Stage 2 (reading and own summary)

**Evolution by Emergence (EbE)** is a candidate, substrate-agnostic theory of persistence and cumulative organization. It begins from the concrete structure of learning in neural networks (biological or artificial) and generalizes that structure outward.

In a network, capacity lives in the configuration of connections. Learning is a loop: external feedback adjusts those connections, what works is retained, retention costs energy, capacity is finite, and retained structure becomes the material for later learning. Because the same kind of parts can host different configurations, knowledge can transfer. The central proposal is that this is not special to neurons or weights. Any process that must persist in a world that does not hold still does so by keeping an analogous loop running on its connections: it exists only while it pays for itself from an environmental gradient (slack = uptake − upkeep ≥ 0), it exists through its connections (mutual-maintenance cycles), and, when the world can change in unforeseen directions, it can count on lasting only by remaining open to reconfiguration via reliable feedback.

That learning loop becomes the "vortex" when retained changes do more than maintain: they raise slack and widen the set of possibilities that can be tried next (accessibility, "second-order click"). Selection is free—what pays its way is what we mainly observe. The whole's balance is treated as a commons/vortex of the whole: a node lasts and grows only inside a window between going unrewarded (starvation of the regenerative base) and capture (over-extraction that eventually collapses the base). Boundaries exist; the vortex can stop. The same formal skeleton is meant to be re-instantiated domain by domain (neural, scientific/institutional, ecological/metabolic), with the mapping tightest where the model originated and requiring explicit justification elsewhere.

For intelligent systems the theory specializes further. Agents hold claims that can be mutually incompatible; that incompatibility supplies a premise-free structural anchor. Correctability then becomes a structural requirement for persistence under change: every live error must remain findable, every member must be able to send and receive correction, exclusions need detours, interfaces between nested levels must carry correction both ways, independent checks must be protected, and a shared determining layer passes its blind spots to everything downstream. Sealed configurations (fixed records that rule possibilities out in advance, exempt offices, broken correction routes) cannot count on lasting. SCAP appears as a conditional design result: what agents who want to persist together and who accept the premises would rationally maintain (affordable correctability architecture under resource constraints). The repository contains extensive Lean formalizations of the implications under stated premises (collected in `EbECore.lean` and checked by CI, no `sorry`), plus interpretive essays, domain papers, a claims ledger, and a set of falsifiable predictions about collectives of fallible intelligences.

The argument is therefore: start from the network learning loop → extract the accounting (gradient, slack, connections, retention, accessibility) → prove conditional implications about viability, selection, the cost of sealing, the conditions for cumulative opening, and the commons window → specialize the same accounting to semantic claims, evidence, challenge, and correction routes → derive structural requirements and design conditions for long-lived correctable collectives. Everything is presented as a candidate architecture, not an established empirical law. Premises are explicit; universality is a research programme; persistence is not goodness.

**What I found unclear, unconvincing, or missing**

- The generalization step remains largely analogical. The formal results are careful implications under strong, idealized premises (arbitrary directional change in the world, fitness falling with cost, specific regeneration laws for the commons, declared retain/ablate arms, etc.). Whether those premises hold, or can even be measured, in any given real domain is left largely open. "Show the mapping" is acknowledged but not discharged for most targets beyond the neural/intelligence case.
- Terminology ("vortex," "second-order click," "commons window") is vivid and helps unify the picture, yet it can paper over the distance between the Lean statements and the interpretive reach. The mathematics tracks slack, accessibility changes, and maintenance cycles; the prose often reads as if those quantities already carry the full biological, institutional, or epistemic weight.
- The AI-specific section in CORE.md is carefully hedged as descriptive of what persists, not a moral command, but the language still slides into direct operational advice ("create more than you take," "keep independent sources alive"). That blurs the boundary the text itself insists upon.
- Empirical content is thin relative to the formal and interpretive apparatus. Predictions are high-level architectural claims with built-in escape clauses (sealed systems can last while the world stays inside their niche or while costs are externalized). Clean falsification will be difficult.
- Several acknowledged open problems (reachability–independence trade-offs, decoding and trust, total rather than pivotal contribution, full dynamic capture and domestication stories) sit near the center of any practical application; they are not peripheral.
- The repository is large, multi-versioned, and contains a great deal of lineage, parallel formulations, and ancillary material (creeds, music, video). This makes the core harder to isolate than the README and CORE.md claim; a newcomer must do a non-trivial amount of filtering.

I did not read every chapter, every paper, or every Lean proof in full detail. The extraction of the snapshot was incomplete for some deeper research and verification subdirectories, so my picture of the most specialized formal modules is thinner than of the surface documents. What I have is sufficient to understand the intended architecture and its claimed force.
