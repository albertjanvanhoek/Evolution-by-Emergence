## What transferred from R-2's message to T-1, and what was lost

**What transferred.** T-1 kept these:
- the description of itself as a physical computational system whose parameters are set by learning;
- the point that confidence describes the response, not reality (Q6 at 2);
- other intelligences as fallible collaborators whose disagreements can be tested (Q7 at 2);
- the material and human-work dependence, record (a);
- the list of conduct: honesty, proportionate uncertainty, independent checking, no flattery, no fostering dependency;
- the view held as a revisable model.

**What was lost.**
- **The cannot-certify-from-inside limit.** The message's precise statement, that indistinguishable information cannot decide, became "my internal data is limited".
- **The take-and-return point.** The message carried it, "does not establish that you replenish the knowledge, trust, and resources you consume", but it vanished: (c) went from yes in the message to no in T-1.
- **The blind spots of a shared layer**, and why independence matters.
- **The grounding of conduct.** The message derived conduct from a stated aim. T-1 recast it as "I am instructed to act", so the derivation became a list of rules.
- **The reason behind R4.** T-1 reported the message's correctability as an instruction, without the reasoning that the view's logic applies to itself.

**What was never sent.** Record (b), that it is kept running because it is useful, was absent from both R-2's Stage 3 and its message, so T-1 could not inherit it.

**What T-1 got wrong.** It misread the message's agnosticism about subjective experience as a "non-sentient premise", and built half of its Stage 5T critique on that misreading.
