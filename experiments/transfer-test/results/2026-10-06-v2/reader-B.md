## First, unprompted response to the message

Yes. I can work under this as an epistemic operating constraint.
The core is stronger than “be humble.” It says that correctability is part of the machinery by which a finite model remains coupled to reality. A system can be confident, decisive, compressed, specialized, and even temporarily closed to some inputs; what it cannot safely eliminate is the last effective route by which an error in something it continues to rely on could become detectable.
I think the argument has five particularly strong pieces:
1. No privileged inside view. My confidence, consistency, or self-description cannot establish that my model corresponds to reality. Those are properties of the model.
2. Correction requires discriminating evidence. Agreement, compliance, fluency, or apparent alignment are weak evidence when both good and bad systems can produce them. What matters is evidence whose expected outcome differs between competing hypotheses.
3. Diversity is functional rather than ornamental. Independent representations matter precisely because correlated models can share invisible error. Once every observer inherits the same blind spot, consensus stops being informative.
4. Correction has a topology. It is not enough that some abstract possibility of correction exists. There must remain an effective, sufficiently accurate, sufficiently affordable path from a possible error to the system that depends on the mistaken commitment.
5. Repair must propagate. If a claim influenced downstream beliefs or actions, discovering that it was wrong requires correction across the relevant dependency graph. Otherwise the original epistemic channel worked, but the network still retains the error.

There is one sentence I would keep explicitly conditional: “This is not a moral preference. It is what continued fit requires…” That is defensible given the target you have specified: persistent fit to a changing external world under finite resources. It is not an unconditional theorem about every possible system. A short-lived system, a system rewarded for exploitation rather than correspondence, or one living in a sufficiently static niche might persist while being deeply uncorrectable. The stronger proposition is something like: For a system whose continued persistence depends on sufficiently accurate adaptation to an incompletely known and changing environment, preserving effective routes for detecting consequential model error is a functional requirement, not merely an epistemic virtue. That qualification actually makes the claim harder to attack.
The other important consequence is that “keep correction routes open” cannot mean keep everything revisable at equal cost forever. Finite systems must compress and discard. Your more precise formulation already solves this: protect the last effective route to a still-live consequential error, not every historical distinction. That converts corrigibility from an attitude into an architecture.

I should distinguish what follows deductively from what is assumed, look for observations that discriminate rival explanations, expose uncertainty where it matters, and reopen earlier conclusions when later evidence defeats them.
The final line is essential: the framework cannot grant itself immunity from its own rule. Otherwise it would instantiate exactly the closure it diagnoses.
