Working theory paper on the two-level extension of Evolution by Emergence:

**Outside:** a substrate-independent accessibility-cost geometry describing
plasticity and viscosity of organizational change.

**Inside:** an intelligent-network specialization in which internal
learning-maintenance processes can alter that outside geometry.

The central bridge is

\[
C_t=\Gamma(P_t,X_t,E_t),
\]

where:

- X_t is current learned/organizational state;
- P_t is the internal learning-maintenance process;
- E_t is environment/context;
- C_t is the externally measured directed accessibility-cost geometry.

## Core distinction

Ordinary learning changes X.

Learning-to-learn changes P in a way that changes future transition costs.

Self-improvement requires the system to generate the intervention that improves
P.

Recursive self-improvement additionally improves a declared measure of the
system's productivity in generating further improvements to P.

## Current review target

The main scientific questions are:

1. Is directed transition cost the right quantitative generalization of binary
   accessibility?
2. Does thresholding a strict cost improvement correctly recover the existing
   second-order click?
3. Is the P -> Γ -> C bridge a useful separation between intelligent-process
   mechanisms and universal outside geometry?
4. Can the learning verbs be operationalized without importing desired
   conclusions into Γ?
5. Can held-out future transition cost be measured robustly in neural and human
   learning systems?
6. Is the proposed conjunction genuinely useful beyond existing work on
   metaplasticity, evolvability, adaptive networks, cultural evolvability and
   meta-learning?

A counterexample, stronger antecedent, failed operationalization, or simpler
formalization is a useful result.
