# Literature positioning

## Status

This is a targeted positioning note, not yet a systematic review.

The paper should not claim priority for metaplasticity, evolvability,
state/topology coevolution, cultural evolvability, meta-learning, or the
stability–plasticity problem. Each is an established literature.

The plausible contribution under review is the conjunction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{directed quantitative accessibility geometry}
+
\text{retained second-order change}
+
\text{inside/outside bridge for intelligent networks}
}
\]

within the broader resource-and-retention architecture of Evolution by
Emergence.

## 1. Metaplasticity

Abraham (2008) defines metaplasticity as persistent activity-induced changes
that affect subsequently induced synaptic plasticity. This is a direct
substrate-specific antecedent for the proposition that present state changes
future change propensity.

Reference:

Abraham, W. C. (2008). Metaplasticity: tuning synapses and networks for
plasticity. Nature Reviews Neuroscience 9, 387.
doi:10.1038/nrn2356.

Relationship to this paper:

Metaplasticity changes future synaptic plasticity. The present framework
abstracts from synapses to a directed cost geometry over organizational
transitions.

## 2. Evolvability

Wagner and Altenberg (1996) argue that evolvability depends on how genetic
variation maps into phenotypic variation, and distinguish the propensity to
vary from realized variation.

Reference:

Wagner, G. P., & Altenberg, L. (1996). Perspective: complex adaptations and the
evolution of evolvability. Evolution 50(3), 967–976.
doi:10.1111/j.1558-5646.1996.tb02339.x.

Barnett, Meister and Rainey (2025) experimentally show that enhanced
evolvability can itself evolve under lineage-level selection.

Reference:

Barnett, M., Meister, L., & Rainey, P. B. (2025). Experimental evolution of
evolvability. Science 387(6736), eadr2756.
doi:10.1126/science.adr2756.

Relationship to this paper:

Evolvability treats the distribution of future adaptive variation as a property
of current organization. Quantitative recursive accessibility treats future
transition costs as the common outside object.

## 3. Adaptive networks

Gross and Blasius (2008) review adaptive networks in which node states and
network topology mutually affect one another.

Reference:

Gross, T., & Blasius, B. (2008). Adaptive coevolutionary networks: a review.
Journal of the Royal Society Interface 5(20), 259–271.
doi:10.1098/rsif.2007.1229.

Relationship to this paper:

Adaptive-network theory establishes state/interaction coevolution. The present
framework asks how those changes alter the cost distribution of future
organizational transitions.

## 4. Exploration, exploitation and network structure

Lazer and Friedman (2007) show in a model of complex problem solving that
efficient information diffusion can improve short-run performance yet harm
long-run exploration by eroding diversity.

Reference:

Lazer, D., & Friedman, A. (2007). The network structure of exploration and
exploitation. Administrative Science Quarterly 52(4), 667–694.
doi:10.2189/asqu.52.4.667.

Barkoczi and Galesic (2016) show that social-learning strategies modify the
effect of communication-network structure on collective performance.

Reference:

Barkoczi, D., & Galesic, M. (2016). Social learning strategies modify the
effect of network structure on group performance. Nature Communications 7,
13109. doi:10.1038/ncomms13109.

Relationship to this paper:

These results argue against treating connectivity or fast diffusion as
monotonically beneficial and support a state/process-dependent viscosity
picture.

## 5. Cultural evolvability

Schimmelpfennig et al. identify sociality, transmission fidelity and cultural
trait diversity as interacting levers of innovation and introduce cultural
evolvability as a framework for their trade-offs.

Reference:

Schimmelpfennig, R., Razek, L., Schnell, E., & Muthukrishna, M. (2022).
Paradox of diversity in the collective brain. Philosophical Transactions of
the Royal Society B 377(1843), 20200316.
doi:10.1098/rstb.2020.0316.

Baumann, Czaplicka and Rahwan (2024) show that the effect of skill diversity on
collective learning depends on network density and task complexity.

Reference:

Baumann, F., Czaplicka, A., & Rahwan, I. (2024). Network structure shapes the
impact of diversity in collective learning. Scientific Reports 14, 2491.
doi:10.1038/s41598-024-52837-3.

Relationship to this paper:

These are especially close antecedents for the inside human-network
specialization. The present paper should not claim that sociality, fidelity or
diversity are newly identified variables.

## 6. Meta-learning

Finn, Abbeel and Levine (2017) explicitly optimize model parameters so new
tasks can be learned with few gradient steps and little data.

Reference:

Finn, C., Abbeel, P., & Levine, S. (2017). Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning for
Fast Adaptation of Deep Networks. Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 70,
1126–1135.

Relationship to this paper:

Meta-learning provides an engineered demonstration that current parameter state
can be optimized for future learnability. The present framework abstracts this
as a change in future transition cost rather than a specific algorithm.

## 7. Plasticity loss

Dohare et al. (2024) show that standard deep-learning systems can progressively
lose their capacity to learn in continual-learning settings even while they
remain trained systems. Their experiments also show that maintaining diversity
can preserve plasticity.

Reference:

Dohare, S., Hernandez-Garcia, J. F., Lan, Q., Rahman, P., Mahmood, A. R., &
Sutton, R. S. (2024). Loss of plasticity in deep continual learning. Nature
632, 768–774. doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07711-7.

Relationship to this paper:

This provides a strong empirical reason to separate current competence from
future learnability and to allow the accessibility geometry to worsen as well as
improve.

## Working novelty boundary

The paper should currently claim:

1. a quantitative cost-based lift of the existing EbE binary
   second-order-accessibility formalism;
2. an explicit outside/inside dual description;
3. a formal interface from intelligent learning-maintenance process state to
   substrate-independent accessibility geometry;
4. operational definitions that distinguish learning, learning-to-learn,
   endogenous self-improvement and recursive self-improvement;
5. a cross-substrate experimental programme based on held-out future transition
   costs.

It should not yet claim that this exact conjunction is absent from all prior
literature. That requires a systematic search focused specifically on
cost-geometric formulations of evolvability, learnability and adaptive
networks.
