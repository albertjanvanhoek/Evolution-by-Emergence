# Literature Positioning — Maintaining the Edges of Learning

## Purpose

This note records the closest neighboring literatures and the intended novelty boundary for the paper.

The paper does **not** claim priority for the individual mechanisms it uses. Its candidate contribution is the integration of:

\[
\text{inside learning-maintenance processes}
\rightarrow
\text{outside edge/accessibility geometry}
\rightarrow
\text{future correction cost}
\rightarrow
\text{retained collective-learning velocity}.
\]

The scale is fixed at a network of intelligent agents.

---

## 1. Exploration, exploitation, and network structure

March (1991) established the general exploration/exploitation tension in organizational learning.

Lazer and Friedman (2007) showed that network structure changes collective exploration and exploitation: efficient information diffusion can improve short-term performance while reducing long-run discovery on complex landscapes.

Barkoczi and Galesic (2016) showed that the effect of network structure depends on the social-learning strategy used by agents.

Brackbill and Centola (2020) provided experimental evidence that less efficient communication networks can outperform more efficient ones on complex problem solving because slower diffusion can preserve independent exploration.

Baumann, Czaplicka and Rahwan (2024) further showed that diversity interacts with network density and task complexity.

**Boundary:** the present paper does not claim that connectivity is beneficial or harmful in general. It asks how maintaining or losing particular informative edges changes future correction accessibility.

---

## 2. Collective intelligence and the collective brain

Woolley et al. (2010) showed that groups display a measurable collective-intelligence factor not reducible to the maximum or average intelligence of members.

Muthukrishna and Henrich (2016) framed cumulative cultural innovation through the “collective brain”: socially distributed knowledge exceeds individual cognitive capacity.

Schimmelpfennig et al. (2022) developed cultural evolvability as an interaction between sociality, transmission fidelity, and cultural diversity.

**Boundary:** the present paper focuses on the dynamic maintenance and repair of the interaction channels through which distributed knowledge can continue to correct the network.

---

## 3. Psychological safety and learning behavior

Edmondson (1999) linked psychological safety in work teams to learning behaviors such as asking for help, discussing errors, seeking feedback, and experimenting.

Edmondson and Lei (2014) reviewed psychological safety as an interpersonal construct affecting voice, learning, and performance.

**Boundary:** edge viscosity is not defined as psychological safety. Psychological safety is a specific psychological/team construct. Edge viscosity is an operational cost abstraction that could be affected by psychological safety, power, communication norms, incentives, prior conflict, or other mechanisms. The relation is empirical.

---

## 4. Conflict and disagreement

De Dreu and Weingart (2003) found negative average associations between both relationship conflict and task conflict and team performance and satisfaction, cautioning against simple claims that disagreement is beneficial.

Subsequent conflict research emphasizes moderators, conflict types, and the frequent entanglement of task and relational conflict.

**Boundary:** the paper does not claim that conflict improves learning. It claims that premature elimination of potentially informative alternatives can remove future search directions, and that L0 disagreement need not automatically destroy L1 correction channels.

---

## 5. Trust violation and repair

Lewicki and Brinsfield (2017) review trust repair after violations, including apologies, explanations, compensation, structural arrangements, monitoring, forgiveness, and other strategies.

Kähkönen (2021) studied team-level trust repair after organizational change and identified communication, collaboration, information sharing, and ethical conduct as relevant practices.

Sala and Pratt (2022) studied organizational practices supporting interpersonal trust repair and reintegration.

**Boundary:** the present paper does not introduce trust repair. It asks a different functional question: what happens to **future learning accessibility and correction velocity** when an information channel is permanently closed, imperfectly restored, or conditionally re-engaged?

---

## 6. Metaplasticity, continual learning, and loss of plasticity

Metaplasticity research shows that prior activity can change the future susceptibility of synapses to plastic change.

Continual-learning research separates retention of prior competence from the ability to continue learning. Dohare et al. (2024) showed that deep networks can progressively lose plasticity over continual training.

**Boundary:** these results operate at a different scale from the present paper. They motivate the structural distinction:

\[
\text{current competence}
\neq
\text{future learnability}.
\]

The present paper applies that distinction to interaction networks among agents, not to synapses or parameters.

---

## 7. Adaptive and temporal networks

Adaptive-network theory studies mutual feedback between node states and network topology. Temporal-network research studies the timing and persistence of edges.

**Boundary:** the paper's specific focus is not topology alone. It introduces a directed, target-dependent **cost of future correction** and links inside agent processes to changes in that cost.

---

## 8. Closest candidate contribution

The strongest candidate contribution is therefore not any individual verb, nor plasticity, network diversity, trust repair, or collective learning alone.

It is the conjunction:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{intelligent agents use learning-maintenance processes}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{those processes alter edge availability, fidelity, repairability, and delay}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{the network's future correction-cost geometry changes}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{retained functional-learning velocity changes}.
\end{array}}
\]

A further conceptual contribution is the explicit separation of:

- **L0:** what the network currently believes or can do;
- **L1:** whether the channels required for later correction remain usable;
- **L2:** whether the network can improve its own L1 processes.

This allows a disagreement about content to be distinguished formally from destruction of the infrastructure needed to investigate future disagreements.
