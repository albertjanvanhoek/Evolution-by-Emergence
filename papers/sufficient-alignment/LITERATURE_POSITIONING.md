# Literature positioning


## Collective intelligence and network topology

Woolley et al. (2010) reported evidence for a group-level collective-intelligence factor across diverse human-group tasks. The present paper does not claim to discover collective intelligence as a phenomenon.

Becker, Brackbill & Centola (2017) showed experimentally that social-network interaction can improve crowd accuracy under conditions where conventional accounts would worry that social influence destroys independence. Centola (2022) reviews the broader network science of collective problem solving and crowd judgment, emphasizing that network topology can help or harm different forms of collective intelligence.

**Novelty consequence:** the present framework should not say that communication, diversity, decentralization, or group cognition are new. Its proposed contribution is to connect the quality and persistence of the interaction architecture to Organizational Accessibility, explicit functional thresholds, repair, and intergenerational protocol retention.


## Maintenance reproduction and spectral thresholds

The spectral-radius threshold used to interpret the maintenance-reproduction extension is established prior mathematics, not a new theorem of this project.

Diekmann, Heesterbeek & Metz (1990) introduced a reproduction-ratio construction for heterogeneous infectious-disease systems in which the dominant eigenvalue of a next-generation operator determines the reproduction threshold. Van den Driessche & Watmough (2002) developed the next-generation-matrix method for general compartmental disease models and showed that the resulting reproduction number is a threshold parameter for local stability. Diekmann, Heesterbeek & Roberts (2010) clarified construction of next-generation matrices and the connection between the dominant eigenvalue, the threshold at one, and early exponential growth. Thieme (2009) placed the relation between spectral bound and reproduction number in a broader positive-operator framework, including discrete-time and infinite-dimensional cases.

Canonical references:

- Diekmann, O., Heesterbeek, J. A. P., & Metz, J. A. J. (1990). On the definition and the computation of the basic reproduction ratio R0 in models for infectious diseases in heterogeneous populations. *Journal of Mathematical Biology*, 28, 365–382. DOI: 10.1007/BF00178324.
- van den Driessche, P., & Watmough, J. (2002). Reproduction numbers and sub-threshold endemic equilibria for compartmental models of disease transmission. *Mathematical Biosciences*, 180, 29–48. DOI: 10.1016/S0025-5564(02)00108-6.
- Diekmann, O., Heesterbeek, J. A. P., & Roberts, M. G. (2010). The construction of next-generation matrices for compartmental epidemic models. *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*, 7(47), 873–885. DOI: 10.1098/rsif.2009.0386.
- Thieme, H. R. (2009). Spectral Bound and Reproduction Number for Infinite-Dimensional Population Structure and Time Heterogeneity. *SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics*, 70(1), 188–211. DOI: 10.1137/080732870.

Perron–Frobenius theory for nonnegative matrices supplies the more general background: irreducible/recurrent blocks have a nonnegative dominant eigenvalue and reducible systems can be decomposed into strongly connected blocks. Circuit and walk representations of nonnegative matrices likewise make clear that closed directed routes contribute to the Perron root. None of those facts is claimed as novel here.

The Lean package therefore stops deliberately short of claiming a machine-checked arbitrary-network spectral theorem. It proves only exact finite algebraic witnesses for a dyad and a directed three-cycle. The general normalized operator

\[
G=(I-R)^{-1}K
\]

and its threshold interpretation via \(\rho(G)\) are treated as external classical mathematics.

**Novelty consequence:** the potentially distinctive contribution is the mapping of this established reproduction mathematics onto a declared *corrective/adaptive maintenance capacity*, together with the separation between (i) reproduction of the maintenance architecture and (ii) externally validated task performance \(\Gamma_H\). In other words, the framework does not claim to invent spectral thresholds; it proposes that collective learning infrastructure can be analysed with the same threshold logic while retaining an independent criterion of whether the reproduced process remains corrigible and useful.


## Signal fidelity

David Blackwell's comparison of experiments is the direct mathematical antecedent. In the Blackwell order, a garbling cannot improve attainable expected utility across decision problems; the converse characterizes the informativeness order under the theorem's assumptions.

Blackwell, D. (1953). Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments. Annals of Mathematical Statistics, 24(2), 265-272. DOI: 10.1214/aoms/1177729032.

The Lean theorem in this package proves only the finite deterministic policy-lifting direction.

## Redundant correction

The \(k\)-out-of-\(n\) system is standard reliability theory. For identical independent components its reliability is a binomial tail. The 2-out-of-3 specialization is used because it yields transparent exact results.

Koucky, M. (2003). Exact reliability formula and bounds for general k-out-of-n systems. Reliability Engineering & System Safety, 82(2), 229-231. DOI: 10.1016/S0951-8320(03)00126-1.

The contribution here is not the reliability formula; it is the interpretation of component reliability as reliability of corrective interaction and the use of a network-level threshold to distinguish sufficient from perfect alignment.

## Protocol inheritance

The threshold for Galton-Watson branching processes is classical: except for degenerate cases, mean offspring at or below one implies extinction almost surely, while mean above one yields a positive survival probability.

The Lean formalization proves only the scalar reproduction threshold and expected-count recurrence. It does not formalize the Galton-Watson extinction theorem.

## Repair and forgiveness

Repeated-game research has already shown that noise can make leniency and forgiveness important for sustaining cooperation.

Fudenberg, D., Rand, D. G., & Dreber, A. (2012). Slow to Anger and Fast to Forgive: Cooperation in an Uncertain World. American Economic Review, 102(2), 720-749. DOI: 10.1257/aer.102.2.720.

Lenaerts and colleagues studied apology and forgiveness in repeated cooperative agreements and derived parameter regions in which forgiveness helps preserve cooperation after mistakes.

The one-step repair inequality in this paper is not presented as a replacement for that richer literature. It isolates a minimal value-of-repair boundary.

## Strategic information caveat

Blackwell-style information dominance assumes a declared decision objective and does not imply that full disclosure is always optimal in strategic environments. Bayesian persuasion and strategic-information design study cases in which senders and receivers have different objectives.

Therefore the proposed honesty interpretation is explicitly limited to cooperative corrective edges with a declared common task objective.

## Proposed contribution

The new claim is architectural rather than mathematical:

> signal fidelity, redundant correction, repairability, recurrent maintenance, and intergenerational protocol persistence can be treated as maintenance dimensions of one collective-intelligence viability framework, connected to Organizational Accessibility and cumulative learning, while maintenance reproduction remains distinct from external correctness.

That synthesis should be evaluated separately from the classical theorems it uses.
