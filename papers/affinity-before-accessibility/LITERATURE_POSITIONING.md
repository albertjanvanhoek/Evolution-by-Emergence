# Literature positioning

## Sabatier principle

The closest established analogue for the turnover channel is the Sabatier principle: catalytic activity is often maximized at intermediate interaction strength because binding must be strong enough for productive reaction but weak enough for turnover and release.

A directly relevant biochemical example is:

Kari J, Olsen JP, Jensen K, et al. **Sabatier Principle for Interfacial (Heterogeneous) Enzyme Catalysis.** ACS Catalysis. 2018. DOI: 10.1021/acscatal.8b03547.

That study reports volcano behavior for cellulase activity as a function of binding strength and explicitly interprets it through the Sabatier principle.

The paper should also acknowledge that volcano relations are not universal mechanistic laws. Critical reviews of electrocatalytic volcano relations emphasize that the Sabatier picture can be incomplete or descriptor-dependent. The present turnover law is therefore an intentionally minimal sufficient model, not a claim that every association process follows the same curve.

## Biological adhesion

Biological adhesion literature distinguishes encounter/contact, interface bonding, mechanical separation, and active remodeling. These mechanisms support the conceptual separation between encounter and association persistence but do not imply the specific reduced equations used here.

Nonequilibrium biomolecular adhesion can also display self-stabilization under load through molecular-state exchange and recruitment, illustrating that association persistence can itself be dynamical rather than a fixed pairwise force.

## Nonequilibrium maintenance cost

The first mechanism should be positioned cautiously. Nonequilibrium states and active localization can require continuing energetic dissipation, but passive bonds do not generally require continuous energetic payment merely to remain bound.

Therefore the linear cost law

\[
\kappa(a)=ca
\]

represents maintained association infrastructure or active association overhead, not attraction in general.

## Proposed contribution

The novelty claim should remain narrow:

1. locate association persistence explicitly before the production matrix in the Organizational Accessibility causal stack;
2. separate a cost-generated association optimum from a turnover-generated association optimum;
3. derive closed-form optimum and viability threshold for the linear-overhead model;
4. machine-check the reduced algebra and an exact no-upkeep turnover witness;
5. connect the turnover mechanism, not the cost mechanism, to the Sabatier principle.

The mathematics of volcano curves and intermediate binding optima is established elsewhere. The contribution is their integration into the resource-limited organizational-accessibility framework.


## Empirical symmetry and asymmetry

The minimal turnover law

\[
h(a)=\frac{4a}{(1+a)^2}
\]

is exactly reciprocal-symmetric:

\[
h(a)=h(1/a).
\]

This is a deliberately strong null prediction. It should not be presented as a generic feature of catalytic volcanoes.

Published catalytic systems can show mechanistically informative asymmetry. Li et al. (2017), **Asymmetric Volcano Trend in Oxygen Reduction Activity of Pt and Non-Pt Catalysts: In Situ Identification of the Site-Blocking Effect**, JACS, DOI 10.1021/jacs.6b11072, report an asymmetric ORR volcano associated with different limiting contributions across the descriptor range.

Volcano plots can also be partitioned into regions controlled by different potential- or rate-determining steps. This supports interpreting deviations from the minimal reciprocal symmetry as evidence for omitted kinetic structure rather than treating them as noise.

For empirical testing, Suvarna et al. (2025), **SPOCK Tool for Constructing Empirical Volcano Diagrams from Catalytic Data**, ACS Catalysis, DOI 10.1021/acscatal.5c00412, provide a standardized framework for fitting empirical volcano relations, quantifying fit quality, handling noisy data, and detecting false-positive volcano claims.

A current enzymatic review is Chiba & Ooka, **How to enhance enzymatic reaction rates? The Sabatier principle and beyond**, DOI 10.1093/bbb/zbaf181, published online in December 2025 and appearing in the 2026 journal volume. It emphasizes optimization of binding-related kinetic parameters rather than monotonic minimization.

These papers create a direct empirical route for the affinity note:

1. fit a volcano relation to measured descriptor-performance data;
2. test reciprocal/log-affinity symmetry as the minimal null;
3. quantify systematic asymmetry;
4. relate asymmetric branches to distinct limiting mechanisms.

The present paper does not yet fit such data. It identifies the testable prediction.
