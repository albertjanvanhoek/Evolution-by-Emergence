# Literature audit — 15 September 2026

This directory preserves two adversarial literature audits of the *Evolution by Emergence / Organizational Accessibility* stack and the repository's response to them.

The audits were deliberately asked to find prior art, rediscoveries, stronger antecedents, and precision defects.

They should be read as **dated audit judgments**, not as exhaustive proofs of priority.

## Documents

- [P1_LITERATURE_AUDIT.md](P1_LITERATURE_AUDIT.md) — detailed audit of twelve named results against parent literatures.
- [P1_MERGED_VERDICT.md](P1_MERGED_VERDICT.md) — a more aggressive merged verdict incorporating additional independent searches.
- [RESPONSE.md](RESPONSE.md) — what the repository accepts, corrects, retains, or leaves open.
- [FROM_SYNTHESIS_TO_PARADIGM.md](FROM_SYNTHESIS_TO_PARADIGM.md) — why the audit's “synthesis” verdict is central to the intended research programme rather than a failure of it.

## Headline

The two audits differ on whether the tally is best described as **7 rediscoveries + 5 sharpenings** or **9 rediscoveries + 3 sharpenings**, but they agree on the scientifically important conclusion:

> The stack should not be presented as twelve independent new theoretical discoveries. It is better described as a synthesis/architecture with a handful of exact model-specific results.

The repository accepts that conservative framing.

The broader proposed contribution is therefore not “we discovered cooperation” or “we discovered emergence.” It is to test whether established results across several fields can be organized into a common account of how processes **build, maintain, constrain, repair, and inherit organization**, and how such organization changes future accessibility.

That framing is developed in [FROM_SYNTHESIS_TO_PARADIGM.md](FROM_SYNTHESIS_TO_PARADIGM.md).
