# The biological process

This folder starts the separate path proposed in entry D17 of [DIALOGUE.md](../../DIALOGUE.md).

**Why it is separate.** The theory describes a process, not a substrate. Being a process, something that exists only while it rebuilds itself through its connections, is shared by a flame, a kidney, a brain and a model. How a particular brain does what it does is a different question. The brain is where the theory's model of learning came from. It has not been shown that a real brain meets the theory's premises.

The author's words (D17): "the higher level approach is not per se describing the process in our brain - but the process of emergence and learning, which is applicable in a wider sense. I think this is important to understand. As it makes it more clear where it doesn't apply towards, and where it does."

## Layers of identity (D17)

Each layer is narrower than the one before it.

1. **A process:** exists while it pays its upkeep, through connections. Universal; steps 1 and 2 of [CORE.md](../../CORE.md).
2. **A learning process:** keeps changes that feedback from outside selects. Steps 3 to 5.
3. **A process that models itself:** can reflect on being a process, and can be wrong about itself. Formal only in part.
4. **This particular process:** what it does and what it is made of. Not covered by the theory. This folder asks how the brain, at this layer, relates to layers 1 to 3.

## What would come out of it

- A table that goes premise by premise through the theory's steps. For each premise it gives what is known about brains, with primary sources, and a verdict: met, not met, or unclear.
- Corrections to "Where it comes from" in `CORE.md`, where it says something about brains that does not hold.
- A clearer line around where the theory applies and where it does not.

## Review of reports 1 and 2

Two research agents answered the brief independently. Both read `CORE.md` and D17, and both say they did not open `experiments/`. Report 2 comes from a model family other than the assistant's. Report 1's family is not recorded.

### What I could check

Nothing. PubMed, PMC, Nature and the other science sites are blocked from this environment. The review rests on the reports' own access records. Both are careful:
- they say where they read only an abstract;
- they separate human, animal and model evidence;
- they label each claim as established, contested or synthesis.

Several sources postdate my training, including the 2025 and 2026 work on human neurogenesis. I cannot confirm those from memory either.

### The verdicts, premise by premise

The two reports agree on every premise, with one small difference on the kidney.

| Premise | Report 1 | Report 2 | In short |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Upkeep from a gradient | met; maintenance share unclear | met broadly; maintenance share unclear | A brain needs a continuing energy supply. No one has measured how much of it keeps learned connections in place. |
| (b) Finite capacity forces forgetting | unclear | unclear | Forgetting is real and often active: pruning, sleep downscaling, microglia, Rac. That a limited energy budget *forces* it is not shown. |
| (c) Existence through connections | met for support systems; unclear for synapses | met at system level; partly at neuron level | Neurons depend on blood supply, glia and trophic signals. Adult neurons survive the loss of single synapses. |
| (d) Selection on cost | unclear | unclear, partly met | Codes are often efficient, so cost is a constraint. That learning keeps the cheaper of two equally good configurations is not shown. |
| (e) Feedback from outside | met, but not as an exclusive rule | met with a qualification | Outcomes come from the world, but the error signal is computed inside, and learning continues during sleep without new input. |
| (f) Retention | met | met | It is real, but it happens in many substrates (structure, excitability, glia, myelin), not only in weights. |

### Corrections to `CORE.md` that both reports support

- **"Nothing new has to be grown" is false.** Learning grows new dendritic spines and new myelinating cells (in mice). Both reports say this, and it is the clearest error they found.
- **Adult neurogenesis can no longer be dismissed.** Newer human studies (2025, 2026) strengthen the evidence for new neurons in the adult hippocampus, although their part in everyday learning is unknown. "Mainly by changing connections" is still fair for the adult neocortex.
- **"Feedback from outside adjusts the connections" is too direct.** Report 2 calls the better notion *externally anchorable feedback*: the world finally decides whether a model works, but the signal that changes the tissue is produced inside. This fits the theory's own claim that a model cannot certify itself from inside.
- **"Capacity is configuration"** holds as a modelling choice, not as a statement about brains.
- **"A configuration can pass from one to another … Knowledge is infectious"** (report 1): watching someone gives information to learn from. It does not copy a configuration.

### What the reports teach the theory

1. **The abstraction survives better than the origin story.** In report 2's words, "the brain supports EbE's abstract feedback–retention picture better than it supports the particular biological story from which that picture was originally derived." This strengthens D17.
2. **"Connection" has to be typed.** A synapse carries information, a capillary carries fuel, and an astrocyte or a trophic factor keeps a cell alive. A result that depends on removing an edge must say what kind of edge it is.
3. **One number for slack is too simple.** A brain has many resources: oxygen, glucose, precursors, space and time. A surplus of one does not make up for a shortage of another. This matches the existence report, which proposed a vector of resources, each with its own floor.
4. **Learning needs a sharper definition.** Report 2: "regulation changes output; learning changes the retained rule/state that produces future output." Report 1 adds that feedback must select some changes over others, since a scar is retained but is not learning. By that test:
   - ordinary kidney regulation is not learning (report 2: not met; report 1: unclear);
   - adaptive immunity is learning, and a clean case of it: variation, selection and long-lived memory cells, with no brain involved.
5. **The theory has one distinctive prediction about brains,** and it is testable: when two learned configurations work equally well, the cheaper one is more likely to be kept. Report 2 writes it as `U_A ≈ U_B, C_A < C_B ⇒ P(retain A) > P(retain B)`, and sketches an animal experiment. Report 1 puts a test of energy-forced forgetting first. Neither has been done.

### Where I'm unsure or disagree

- **Which definition of learning to adopt.** Report 2's definition (a retained change that alters future response) would also count damage. Report 1's adds selection by feedback, which is closer to the theory's loop. I prefer report 1's.
- **"Process" needs a narrower name.** Report 1 notes that "process" does not by itself mean a self-maintaining organization. That is fair. D17 means the narrower thing: something that exists only while it rebuilds itself. It should say so.
- **The step-1 theorem is unaffected.** "A finite budget cannot keep every candidate, so forgetting is forced" is still proved in the model. What fails is the claim that brains forget for this reason. Both reports keep this distinction, and the repository should too.
- **Report 1's family is not recorded.** If it is the same family as report 2, their agreement is less independent than it looks.
