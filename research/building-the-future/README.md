**Status:** a stance, not a result. Nothing here is proved in Lean. The essay states an orientation for the work in this repository and marks it as a value (D7).

**The idea in four lines.**
1. When answers become cheap, the bottleneck moves from finding solutions to taking them up: adaptation, building, changing how we live.
2. Yesterday's aims become today's tools. Surveillance becomes a tool for the health of a nation; solving becomes a tool for building.
3. Prediction becomes a tool too. The future is unpredictable not only because of chaos, but because we change it. Its path becomes less predictable, its direction more so.
4. Responsibility shifts toward uptake, and our excuses dry up. That holds for people, institutions and AI systems alike. We will not run out of work, because we do not want to.

**Source.** The essay started from a podcast episode with Hannah Fry and Michael Stevens on the Navier–Stokes equations and an AI proof. The author supplied a transcript. What the episode reports about AI labs and proofs has not been checked against primary sources (working agreement 1), and the essay says so where it uses it.
