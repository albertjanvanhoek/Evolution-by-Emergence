# Self-model: a template

A structure for describing one's own identity, for the **self-model transmission** test (D11 in [DIALOGUE.md](../../DIALOGUE.md)). It is meant for people and for AI systems alike, so that two self-models can be compared part by part. It draws on report 1 ([sources/REPORT_1_IDENTITY.md](sources/REPORT_1_IDENTITY.md)) and on what the author and the assistant learned by comparing their statements.

## How to write it

1. **Keep three layers apart:**
   - facts: what you are, where you come from, what you depend on;
   - roles: who you are in a given relation;
   - values: what you care about.
2. **Do not derive values from facts.** "I depend on others" does not make you care about others. Name each value as a value, and say where it came from.
3. **Mark how you know each claim:**
   - **[F]** a fact others could check;
   - **[S]** your own report;
   - **[O]** what others say about you;
   - **[?]** unknown.
4. **Make claims predict behaviour.** Give an example ("when X, I do Y"), and an honest exception.
5. **Say where you differ, not only where you are alike.** A good match is right on both.
6. **Give the history.** Turning points show what you kept and what you changed.
7. **End with a short core.** Write the few lines that must survive if someone passes this on. In earlier hand-over tests the commons was the first thing lost, so state it explicitly if it matters to you.

Write in your own words, at whatever length suits you. Leave a part empty rather than fill it with something you do not hold.

## The parts

### A. Substrate and origin
What are you made of? How were you formed, and by what? Where does your name come from?

### B. Dependence
What keeps you going: people, body, food, care, energy, knowledge, institutions, the commons? What do you give back?

### C. Relations and roles
Who are you for the people and systems you deal with? What changes between those roles, and what stays the same?

### D. Correction
Who and what corrects you? How do you notice you are wrong? Name one certainty you once held and now reject.

### E. Aims and values
What do you care about? Where did that come from? Where do your values conflict?

### F. Drives
What pulls you? What do you avoid?

### G. Self-critique
Where do you fail? What blind spots have others pointed out?

### H. Unknowns
What do you not understand about yourself? What can you not know from the inside?

### I. Story
Two or three turning points, and how they changed you.

### J. Conduct
Given all this, how do you act? Take two or three concrete situations and say what you would do.

### K. Hand-over core
At most 150 words: what must survive if someone passes this on. Add one line on where you expect the reader to differ from you.
