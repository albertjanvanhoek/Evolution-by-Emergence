Here is the list, grouped by what each kind of model is for. One rule applies throughout, the one we settled this afternoon. Only the world model aims at nothing except fitting reality. Every other model has an aim. Its aim may steer what gets asked and what gets done, but it never counts as evidence for what is the case.

## 1. Models of what is

These describe and have no aim. Most are the world model zoomed in on one part.

| Model | What it models | How it helps persist | How it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **World model** | Reality and its possible futures, including those that depend on one's own actions | Keeps the intelligence in step with a changing world (step 4); every other model draws on it | Its fit is fixed by something other than evidence, so it seals |
| **Self-model** | Body or substrate, abilities, limits, one's own likely behaviour | Shows what one can actually do and where one breaks | Flattery; it also cannot certify its own reliability from inside, so it needs others |
| **Ledger model** | Uptake, upkeep, slack and reserve, for each resource separately (time, energy, money, health) | Tracks the first condition, that existence is paid for (step 1); shows a deficit before the reserve is gone | A surplus in one resource is taken to cover a deficit in another; or the accounting boundary is drawn after seeing the result |
| **Dependency model** | Who keeps me going, whom I keep going, and the exchanges in both directions | Protects the connections one lives through (step 2); shows what a partner's failure will do to me | Forgets that the support runs both ways; sees itself as self-made |
| **Model of other minds** | What others believe, want and can do, including their world models | Cooperation, anticipation, building bridges (D35), spotting manipulation | Projection; or treating "X wants Y" as evidence that Y is true |
| **Commons model** | The shared stock (language, knowledge, trust, infrastructure), how fast it regenerates, who takes what | Keeps one's taking within the interest; makes capture visible (step 6) | Treats the commons as free or endless; misses the parts that regenerate it (trust, the capacity to check) |
| **Institution model** | Rules, roles, sanctions and reputations as they actually operate | Predicts enforcement; detection and reputation work as routes to correction | Takes the rules as written for the rules as enforced |
| **Source model** | Where evidence comes from, how reliable it is, and which sources depend on each other | Weights feedback, since reliable feedback is the premise of step 4; "count origins, not copies" | Counts copies as confirmations; trusts a channel that shares its own blind spot |
| **Record** | What happened, including what one did and why | Lets one learn from actions whose counterfactual is gone (E9); supports accountability and reputation | Rewrites history to fit the present self |
| **Calibration model** (a model of one's own models) | How often each model is right, and where it is likely wrong | Shows when to check and when to ask others; keeps the intelligence correctable | Treats a feeling of certainty as proof (step 8) |

## 2. Models of what to do

These are directive: each has an aim.

| Model | What it models | How it helps persist | How it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Value model** | What matters and what ought to be | Says what persisting is *for*; persistence is not goodness (D7) | Values pose as facts; or no values are stated, so whatever lasts gets counted as good |
| **Want model** | What I want, now and later | Gives direction and motivation | Wishful thinking: the want overwrites the world model |
| **Action model** | The options, waiting included, under time pressure, weighing what more checking would be worth and whether a step can be undone | Ends the regress at each decision; lets one act before a misfit drains the reserve | Endless deliberation; or acting on urgency someone manufactured |
| **Plan model** | Sequences of steps, contingencies, commitments over time | Coordinates action across time; prepares for possible futures | A plan read as a prediction, and never revised |
| **Attention model** | What to check next, where to look, what listening costs | Spends the learning budget where it pays; listening has to be affordable (step 4) | Looks only where confirmation lies |
| **Revision policy** | How to update, what to keep, what to forget | Runs the learning loop itself: feedback plus retention (step 5); some forgetting is forced by a finite budget | Revision bought by vagueness; forgetting the wrong things; sealing |
| **Commitment model** | Promises and obligations: what others can count on from me | Makes me a partner worth keeping, so the connections hold | Commits beyond what one's slack can carry |
| **Correction-route model** | Who can correct me, how, and which routes are still open | Keeps every live error findable; removing the last route that could reveal an error seals it | Cuts off critics; routes cost upkeep, so they are the first thing dropped |
| **Care model** | Whom I carry and who carries me, within whose margins | Care lasts only while it fits the carer's slack, in every resource | Exhausts the carer; leaves out the respite the carer needs |

## 3. Models of what could be

These explore and claim nothing.

| Model | How it helps persist | How it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Scenarios and counterfactuals** | Lets you rehearse an action in a model before trying it in reality, as public health does with epidemic models | A scenario gets read as a forecast |
| **Imagination** | Produces new combinations: the "generate" factor in the vortex | Without validation it is noise |
| **Play and fiction** | Explores possibilities without paying for real misfit; stories, songs and letters carry models to others | Fiction gets taken as record |
| **Openness to new kinds of possibility** | The only defence against an error that every existing method shares (C4); a fixed space of possibilities saturates (step 7) | It is the hardest model to keep: there is nothing to check it against in advance |

## 4. Models held together

| Model | How it helps persist | How it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared language and concepts** | A precondition for any bridge between models; makes coordination cheap | A shared layer passes on its blind spots to everyone |
| **Shared record with provenance** | Contradictory reports can stand side by side, each attributed to its source | It slides into consensus by default |
| **Consensus belief** | Allows coordinated action | Forced consensus loses calibration and the ability to detect new errors (D35) |
| **Collective decision model** | Lets a group act together under time pressure | Whoever chooses the transfers chooses who fails |
| **Shared narrative** (who we are) | Cohesion, trust, care | Defends itself against evidence |

**Identity** sits across groups 1 and 2. Part of it is fact (the self-model) and part is aim (who I want to be). It does harm when the aim starts defending the facts.

## How the models work together to persist

One turn of the loop looks like this:
1. **Check viability.** The ledger model says whether the intelligence is still viable, and how much reserve it has.
2. **Read the world.** The world model, weighted by the source model, says what is the case and what may happen, including under each available action.
3. **Decide.** The attention model decides what to check first. The action model, informed by values and wants, then chooses under time pressure, with waiting as one option.
4. **Act and record.** The intelligence acts, and the record keeps what was done. The world changes, and so does the evidence.
5. **Revise.** The revision policy updates the models, the calibration model notes what was wrong, and the correction routes let others point out what one cannot see oneself.

That is the learning loop of step 5, spelled out as models. A turn that both raises slack and widens what can be tried is a turn of the vortex.

## What stands out

These are my readings: proposed, not derived.

- **Most failures are leaks between groups.** A want overwrites the world model. Identity overwrites the self-model. Consensus overwrites the record. A plan or a scenario gets read as a forecast. Keeping the boundaries may be the most general rule in the list.
- **Every model costs upkeep.** No intelligence keeps all of them in full detail: purpose sets the scope, and some forgetting is forced. My guess at the ones it can't do without:
  - the world model;
  - the self-model;
  - the ledger model;
  - the dependency model;
  - the action model;
  - correction routes, because the self-model cannot certify itself.
- **Several cannot be done alone.** The reliability of the self-model, a blind spot shared with one's sources, and calibration all need others. That is step 2, "existence through connections", applied to knowing.
- **Persistence enters only through the directive models.** That persisting is the aim is stated, not derived, and persisting is not the same as being good.

Some existing work overlaps with this, as leads I haven't checked in the sources yet:
- Conant and Ashby's "good regulator" theorem, on why a regulator has to model what it regulates;
- the belief–desire–intention model of agents (Bratman), which roughly matches the world, want and plan models here;
- theory of mind;
- predictive processing.
