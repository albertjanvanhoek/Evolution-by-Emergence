# CP projector loops: spatial conditioning with strand wiring

**Research note, 7 November 2026.** A response to the independent review of [CPN_BOUNDARY_COMPARISON.md](CPN_BOUNDARY_COMPARISON.md): for oriented CP index loops, conditioning on the exterior does *not* generally produce the same random loop model on a smaller induced subgraph with free or equally pinned spins. It produces a boundary **wiring/permutation state**. This elementary statement is exact at finite volume, and explains the currently missing step between one-annulus crossing and multi-annulus exponential decay. This note does **not** prove a mass gap or disprove all possible boundary comparisons.

## 1. Exact conditional gluing rule

We use the free-boundary finite-graph \(CP^{q-1}\) projector action from [CPN_POSITIVE_LOOPS.md](CPN_POSITIVE_LOOPS.md), at integer \(q\ge2\), \(\theta=0\), and \(b_e\ge0\). A diagram \(\omega\) consists of integer edge multiplicities \(k_e\) and, at each free vertex \(x\), a permutation \(\pi_x\in S_{d_x}\) pairing the \(d_x=\sum_{e\ni x}k_e\) incoming and outgoing colored-index strands. Its *scalar diagram weight after color summation* is
\[
 w(\omega)=
 \left(\prod_e\frac{b_e^{k_e}}{k_e!}\right)
 \left(\prod_{x}\frac1{(q)_{d_x}}\right)
 q^{\ell(\omega)},
 \qquad (q)_d=q(q+1)\cdots(q+d-1),
 \tag{W1}
\]
where \(\ell(\omega)\) counts closed index loops. This is the Wolff-type positive oriented-loop expansion; see the prior-work discussion in the linked note. We work here with **all spins free**, to keep pin labels out of the first argument.

Choose a region \(\Lambda\) and cut its incident edge strands at a fixed set of ports. Condition on (i) the *number and labels* of all cut-edge strands and (ii) the exterior pairings and paths. Because every line is directed (one incoming and one outgoing color index), the exterior paths connect outgoing ports to incoming ports via a bijection \(\sigma\in S_r\), where \(r\) is the number of matched port pairs. There may also be closed exterior loops, but their factor \(q^{\ell_{\mathrm{out}}}\) is constant under conditioning. Likewise, an interior configuration induces a bijection \(\pi\in S_r\) among the complementary ports, together with \(\ell_{\mathrm{in}}\) loops entirely inside.

Gluing inside to outside creates exactly
\[
\ell(\omega)=\ell_{\mathrm{out}}+\ell_{\mathrm{in}}
+\#\operatorname{cycles}(\sigma\circ\pi).
\tag{W2}
\]
The order/inverse of \(\sigma,\pi\) depends on the port-label convention; the number of cycles is independent of the corresponding consistent relabeling. Thus the conditional interior law, **at the fixed cut-edge multiplicities**, takes the exact form
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathbb P(\omega_{\mathrm{in}}\mid\omega_{\mathrm{out}})
=\frac{w_{\mathrm{local}}(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})\,
 q^{\ell_{\mathrm{in}}+\#\operatorname{cycles}(\sigma\circ\pi)}}
{\displaystyle\sum_{\omega'_{\mathrm{in}}}
 w_{\mathrm{local}}(\omega'_{\mathrm{in}})\,
 q^{\ell'_{\mathrm{in}}+\#\operatorname{cycles}(\sigma\circ\pi')}}.
\quad}
\tag{W3}
\]
Here \(w_{\rm local}\) collects all edge-multiplicity and free-vertex \((q)_{d_x}^{-1}\) factors assigned to the inside, plus factors from already fixed strands; it does **not** include any loop fugacity generated by gluing to the outside.

This is a *domain Markov property on an **enlarged boundary state** \((r,\sigma)\)*. It is **not** an ordinary induced-subgraph DLR law: the factor \(\#\operatorname{cycles}(\sigma\circ\pi)\) couples distant boundary ports. Without the external wiring \(\sigma\), the interior law is not determined.

With two fixed rank-one projector directions \(A,B\), a related tangle rule remains positive upon completing full loops: replace each completed loop's factor \(q\) by \(1\) if it touches only one pinned direction, or \(p^{k_\gamma}\) if it touches both, with \(p=\operatorname{tr}(P_AP_B)\). The wiring state then must **also carry the order/labels of pinned visits** along exterior open paths. For three or more arbitrary pin orientations, a completed loop can have a negative/complex trace; this was the separate obstruction in [CPN_BOUNDARY_COMPARISON.md](CPN_BOUNDARY_COMPARISON.md). Positivity with two pin directions does not remove the need for strand wiring.

## 2. A minimal, exact wiring-sensitivity test

Take two copies of one edge joining a free inside vertex \(u\) to a free outside vertex \(v\) (the \(k_e=2\) term in the expansion on this one-edge graph). Each vertex has \(d=2\), so its permutation can be either \(I\) or \(s=(12)\), *each with the same vertex factor \(1/(q)_2\)*. All edge-multiplicity factors are also identical.

Condition on the exterior vertex permutation \(\sigma\). There are only two possible interior states \(\pi=I,s\), with unnormalized conditional weights
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\pi=I&\pi=s\\\hline
\sigma=I&q^2&q\\
\sigma=s&q&q^2
\end{array}
\tag{W4}
\]
because identical pairings create two loops, while opposite pairings create one loop.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathbb P(\pi=s\mid\sigma=I)=\frac1{q+1},
\qquad
\mathbb P(\pi=s\mid\sigma=s)=\frac q{q+1}.
\quad}
\tag{W5}
\]
For \(q=3\), the same local internal geometry and multiplicities give probabilities \(1/4\) and \(3/4\), respectively. This is a genuine graph/multiplicity term of the **CP projector** loop expansion, not an invented stochastic loop gas. Both external pairings have positive weights.

It shows exactly why one cannot *drop the exterior wiring and claim the interior has the same law as a subgraph with standard free boundary*. It does **not** by itself disprove any correctly formulated, suitably averaged annular inequality. It also does not show non-exponential decay: two strand ports are not a large annulus.

## 3. A quantitative warning about chaining scales

Changing an exterior wiring \(\sigma\) by a single transposition can merge two cycles or split one cycle, so for any **fixed interior diagram** its unnormalized weight changes by a factor \(q\) or \(q^{-1}\). After normalization, a crude change-of-boundary Radon–Nikodym estimate for every interior event \(E\) is
\[
q^{-2}\,\mathbb P_\sigma(E)
\ \le\ \mathbb P_{\sigma'}(E)
\ \le\ q^2\,\mathbb P_\sigma(E)
\tag{W6}
\]
when \(\sigma'\sigma^{-1}\) is one transposition and the conditional local weights and cut multiplicities are the same.

For \(m\) such transpositions, the immediate iteration yields only a factor \(q^{2m}\). Since a macroscopic cut may contain order \(R\) or more strand ports, **a naive comparison could introduce a cost exponential in the boundary size**, swamping the desired \(C_\beta/\log R\) estimate. This is an *upper bound on the crudity of this comparison strategy*, not a proof that the true model incurs such a cost, and not a theorem about the number of ports (which is random and unbounded because multiplicities are unbounded).

The lesson is to seek either a **wiring-uniform** annular estimate for a precisely specified interior *event*, or a switching/reweighting argument that removes the exterior wiring *in expectation* without paying a factor growing with its number of ports. A putative estimate for loops that were already joined outside must be scoped carefully: if the exterior wiring has already connected the marked boundary families, the event “they are connected somewhere” can be deterministic independently of what happens inside. A useful event should isolate connectivity **created by the interior** or use an appropriate conditional marked-strand observable.

## 4. Literature with an explicit relevance ranking

These are examples of technology, **not directly applicable theorems for the CP projectors**.

1. **Wolff (2010), *Simulating the All-Order Strong Coupling Expansion IV: CP(N−1) as a loop model*, arXiv:1001.2231.** The exact CP action, oriented loops and marked-worm correlations. **Start here** for the model's permitted local strand switchings and the exact weights; no need to reinvent the representation.
2. **Benassi & Ueltschi (2019/2020), *Loop Correlations in Random Wire Models*, [Commun. Math. Phys. 374, 525–547](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-019-03474-9).** Random links with vertex pairings, and an explicit treatment of **open loops ending at boundary vertices**, Section 3. Closest geometric bookkeeping analogue, but a different measure.
3. **Lees & Taggi (2021), *Exponential decay of transverse correlations for O(N) spin systems and related models*, [Probab. Theory Relat. Fields 180, 1099–1133](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00440-021-01053-5).** An explicit **colour-switch lemma** for colored paths, valid under particular positive local-weight inequalities. This is the most promising *switching-method* analogue, but those hypotheses must be checked anew for the oriented CP model.
4. **Camia & Lis (2017), *Non-Backtracking Loop Soups and Statistical Mechanics on Spin Networks*, [Ann. Henri Poincaré 18, 403–433](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00023-016-0524-3).** A genuinely proved spatial Markov property upon conditioning on boundary occupation fields, for a different loop-soup model. Shows what sort of boundary state can suffice, not proof of one for CP.
5. **Herdeiro (2017), *Markov chain sampling of the O(n) loop models on the infinite plane*, [Phys. Rev. E 96, 013305](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.96.013305).** Explicit recognition that one must store sufficient information about boundary-crossing loops to factorize an otherwise nonlocal loop-fugacity measure.
6. **Classical Ising random currents and switching lemma:** [Bauerschmidt lecture notes, Section 6.1](https://cims.nyu.edu/~bauerschmidt/teaching/math253x/spin.pdf), and related sources. The symmetric-difference/source-switch structure is **special to Ising currents** and cannot simply be transferred to CP oriented strands.
7. **Duminil-Copin, Glazman, Peled & Spinka (2021), *Macroscopic loops in the loop O(n) model at Nienhuis' critical point*, [J. Eur. Math. Soc. 23, 315–347](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000198186).** Domain gluing and positive association, but for nonintersecting hexagonal-lattice loops and special \(n,x\) ranges; it does not assert CP ferromagnetic/FKG properties.
8. **Taggi & Wu (2026), *Mermin–Wagner Theorem for Dimers, Monomer Double-Dimers, and Spatial Random Permutations*, [Commun. Math. Phys. 407, 175](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-026-05626-0).** Shows how an auxiliary complex-spin representation can yield *vanishing* two-point connectivity for several loop models without a positive spin measure. Their model class, observable, and decay strength must be checked before proposing a CP analogue. Their theorem is **not an all-coupling exponential CP decay proof**.

## 5. A precise next candidate lemma (OPEN)

Fix a planar annulus, \(q\ge2\), and two distinguished families of index strands associated to a marked pair or two equal-pinned boundary layers. Let \(\sigma\) denote exterior strand wiring and \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{new}}\) an interior-created connection event with an explicit definition that ignores already externally completed connections.

> **Wiring comparison / switching problem.** Is there an annular crossing bound, or an averaged identity for the relevant marked-strand observable, which is uniform in the exterior connectivity state \(\sigma\) (possibly under a neutral/compatible subclass), and whose constants do not scale as \(q^{O(\#\text{ports})}\)?

To attack it constructively:
- First test the conditional partition polynomials for **2 and 3 strands** and the action of transpositions on boundary permutations (complete finite enumeration).
- Search for a CP analogue of the Lees–Taggi *colour switch*, e.g. a weight-preserving involution on an augmented pair of diagrams that switches marked paths while controlling \((q)_{d_x}^{-1}\) and loop fugacities.
- Define the correct event and compare it **in an ensemble with fixed cut multiplicities**; only then integrate the cut multiplicities. Conditioning on only a partial exterior would mix states with different numbers of ports.
- If no wiring-uniform lemma exists, try a **two-layer/annealed switching inequality** instead of a worst-case conditional one, which is likely too strong.

**Logical status:** (W2)–(W5) are exact combinatorics of the model. (W6) is a deliberately crude finite-dimensional comparison. The proposed scale iteration and switching lemma remain **open**, and even a successful estimate would still need to bridge point-pinned and whole-annulus ensembles as in [CPN_BOUNDARY_COMPARISON.md](CPN_BOUNDARY_COMPARISON.md).
