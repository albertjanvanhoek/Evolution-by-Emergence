# The integrity of world models: neurorights as part of human rights

This folder follows up entry D33 in [DIALOGUE.md](../../DIALOGUE.md). It builds a bridge between three things: the neurorights proposed for neurotechnology, the human rights that already exist, and what this repository says about learners that model a reality larger than themselves.

**Status:** a concept note, not a result. Each claim is labelled as in the [creed](../discovarianism/CREED.md): **Follows** (from being a learning process), **If** (given a named aim), or **Proposed** (a value). Every quotation from a legal or UN source was checked in that source on 21 October 2026.

## 1. The author's claim

The author starts from one premise and draws a practical conclusion from it:

> "ALS elk brein de werkelijkheid modelleert in plaats direct toegang heeft tot de werkelijkheid dan moeten we dit in de alledaagse omgang met elkaar als uitgangspunt nemen."

(If every brain models reality rather than having direct access to it, we must take this as the starting point of how we deal with each other every day.)

Then the step that makes it a matter of rights:

> "dan ontneem ik iets van jou als ik bijvoorbeeld tegen je lieg, dan functioneert je model technisch minder goed, wat eigenlijk hetzelfde is als ik via een apparaat je model zal beïnvloeden. Het verloopt alleen via een andere weg - maar is in uitkomst misschien hetzelfde."

(Then I take something from you when I lie to you: your model works less well, which is in effect the same as influencing your model through a device. It only goes by another route, and the outcome may be the same.)

And two further points:
- the influence is mutual, so it brings rights *and* duties;
- the integrity at stake is not only that of one person's model, but "de integriteit van wereldmodellen, maar als collectief" (the integrity of world models, but as a collective).

What is new is the claim that ties these together: that deliberate manipulation by deception and by neural intervention cause the same *kind* of harm, and that this is the harm human rights already protect against.

## 3. One harm, several routes

A world model can be damaged at three places:
- **input:** what reaches it is false or distorted;
- **update:** the way it changes is taken over, so that it changes for reasons it cannot see or check;
- **correction routes:** the routes that could reveal its errors are cut.

Different routes reach these places:

| Route | Input | Update | Correction routes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A neural intervention without consent | | ✓ | ✓ (often leaves no trace the person can find) |
| Deliberate deception aimed at steering | ✓ | ✓ | |
| Concealed steering: microtargeting, curation built to exploit, persuasion the person cannot recognise | ✓ | ✓ | |
| Censorship, removing independent sources, a single shared layer | | | ✓ |

**Not every falsehood is a lie, and not every lie is manipulation.** Most of what people pass on is uncertain, and often nobody knows whether it is true, the speaker included. There is a range:
- **an honest error:** passed on in good faith. The receiver's model gets worse, but nothing was taken on purpose. The answer is correction and repair, not blame (creed 2 and 3);
- **an uncertain claim:** neither side knows. The duty is to mark the uncertainty;
- **a known falsehood:** the speaker knows it is false;
- **deliberate manipulation:** deception or concealment used on purpose to steer the other's beliefs or decisions.

The author's equivalence is claimed only at the last step: when deception is deliberate and aimed at steering, it is as harmful as any other manipulation, whatever the route. Before that step, the harm to a model can be real while the wrong is small or absent.

**The criterion (proposed).** An influence on another learner is impermissible when it is exercised knowingly and degrades that learner's capacity to stay in step with reality and to correct itself, without the learner's free and informed consent. The criterion looks at what happens to the model, not at the substrate the influence passes through. Influence that degrades a model by honest error falls outside it; that is what correction is for.

Two parts of this follow; one is proposed:
- *Follows:* a learner fed distorted signals stops tracking (creed 1); a learner whose last correction route is cut is sealed (`last_route_removal_seals`).
- *Proposed:* that degrading another's model is a wrong, and that consent is the line.

**The author's sharpening.** A lie can be as deliberate as a neural intervention: "liegen kan bewust zijn en net zo goed een vorm zijn van manipulatie - precies zoals het aantast van iemand brein zelf." The UN Special Rapporteur on freedom of religion or belief gives case-by-case criteria for when influence on thought becomes impermissible manipulation ([A/76/380](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n21/274/90/pdf/n2127490.pdf), 2021, para. 36): whether consent was free and informed, whether the influence is concealed, whether there is an imbalance of power, and harm as an aggravating factor. Deliberate deception meets the concealment criterion by definition: it works only while the other does not see it. Whether a given case reaches the threshold of a rights violation, the report leaves to case-by-case assessment.

## 4. The bridge: existing rights read as protecting world models

The neurorights came out of a 2017 meeting at Columbia University, published in Nature as four ethical priorities for neurotechnologies and AI ([manuscript copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10092977)). They are now usually stated as five rights: mental privacy, free will, personal identity, protection from discrimination based on brain data, and equal access to neural augmentation ([Columbia Magazine](https://magazine.columbia.edu/article/need-protect-data-our-brains)). The table reads each against an existing right, the repository, and the duty it implies between people.

| Neuroright | Existing human right | Reading in this repository | Duty between people |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mental privacy | UDHR art. 12; ICCPR art. 17; the right not to reveal one's thoughts (A/76/380, para. 26) | A model inferred without consent becomes a target for steering. Privacy keeps the update in the learner's hands. | Do not extract what was not offered. |
| Free will, cognitive liberty | UDHR art. 18 and 19; ICCPR art. 18(2), "no one shall be subject to coercion which would impair his freedom to have or to adopt a religion or belief of his choice"; ICCPR art. 19(1), "the right to hold opinions without interference" | The update stays the learner's own when change comes through evidence it can see and check (creed 2, 6). | Influence only by routes the other can check: evidence and argument, not concealment. |
| Personal identity | UDHR art. 26(2), education "directed to the full development of the human personality"; art. 29(1) | Identity is the path-dependent imprint of what one learned, with agency over the path ahead ([WHERE_DID_YOU_LEARN_THAT.md](../identity/WHERE_DID_YOU_LEARN_THAT.md), creed 9). | Correct one view without attacking the whole person (`revision_keeps_the_rest`). |
| Protection from discrimination | UDHR art. 2 and 7 | Discounting a signal for its source closes a route to correction (creed 10). A category is not a path (`category_does_not_determine_identity`). | Weigh what someone says by its content. |
| Fair access to augmentation | UDHR art. 27(1): "to share in scientific advancement and its benefits"; ICESCR art. 15 | Knowledge is commons; capture eats the principal (`capture_collapses_commons`); access at cost ([transparent-pricing](../transparent-pricing/README.md)). | Take no more than the shared stock renews. |
| **Not yet a neuroright: the integrity of shared world models** | UDHR art. 19, the freedom "to seek, receive and impart information and ideas"; art. 28, a social order in which the rights "can be fully realized"; art. 29(1), "duties to the community"; the fourth attribute of freedom of thought in A/76/380, a state duty to foster an enabling environment (para. 25) | Connected agents share one evidence-defined reality; independent sources are both commons and correction routes; a network corrects only between isolation and full conformity (`correcting_levels_form_interval`). | The relational duties: do not distort, keep the other able to correct you, attempt repair, make room to speak. |

The last row is what the author adds. The five neurorights protect an individual mind against an outside intervention. They say little about the shared reality that minds keep up together, and nothing about the duties of the people who keep it up.

## 5. What rights are for

In the author's words: "the human rights are there for naming the principles and setting boundaries towards the excess." And: "They provide formal structure and boundaries. And they are all human made. They are inventions of our imagination."

This shapes how the note should be read:
- **Rights mark the boundary, not every case.** Most influence between people, including most falsehoods, sits well inside the boundary. It belongs to everyday ethics: correction, repair and the duties of effort. Rights name the principle and draw the line where influence becomes excess, such as deliberate manipulation.
- **The neurorights work the same way as the other human rights.** They give a principle a formal structure and say where it is crossed. That is why they can be read as part of the existing framework rather than beside it.
- **Rights are made.** They are inventions, kept in force by people who use them, teach them and enforce them. In the repository's terms they are part of the commons: shared rules that every node draws on and did not make alone (step 6), and distributed rules in the sense of D27.
- **Made, so correctable.** What is invented can be revised, by linked revision as the creed asks of itself: say what was held, what changed it, and why. Being invented does not make a right arbitrary. *Assistant's reading, proposed:* one test of a right is whether it keeps the network able to correct itself, without forcing it into conformity.

## 6. Autonomous interdependence: why it hangs together

The author's term for what ties this note together is **autonomous interdependence** (see [SHARED_IDENTITY.md](../identity/SHARED_IDENTITY.md)). In his words: "the interdependence is about creating and sustaining autonomy". And: "that is allowing for variation and different perspectives. As that is the idea behind it - that you allow for this by holding up autonomy and interdependence."

**Autonomy is produced by connections.** A model can update itself, check itself and stay in step only if faithful signals keep reaching it from others (`learner_in_step`, `outside_evidence_can_settle`). Its autonomy is made by its connections, not in spite of them. A learner left wholly alone is not free; it is sealed (`isolation_cannot_correct`). *Follows.*

An example from the conversation behind D33: naming one's own emotion removes the dependence on another's acknowledgement, a gain in autonomy. But the words and distinctions needed to name it were learned from others. Self-validation is autonomy that interdependence built.

**The two halves of the bridge protect the two halves of the term.**
- **The neurorights and freedom of thought protect autonomy:** the inner model, and the learner's own way of updating it.
- **The relational duties protect the interdependence that produces it:** faithful signals, staying correctable, repair, room to speak.

Without the second, the first has nothing to stand on. Without the first, the second becomes control.

**What both protect is variation.** Autonomy keeps perspectives different; interdependence keeps them in contact. Correction needs both. A shared blind spot is corrected only by a node that is independent of the shared layer and still connected to the rest, and a network corrects only between isolation and full conformity (`hub_determined_network_inherits_blind_spot`, `correcting_levels_form_interval`). So different perspectives are not noise to be removed. They are the network's routes to correction. *Follows,* for correction. That variation should also be valued for its own sake is *proposed*.

**Interdependence does not build autonomy by itself.** It can also wear it down: through capture, through dependence that serves one side, through pressure to conform. Whether it builds autonomy depends on the quality of the relationships. That is why the duty to the quality of relationships (D28) is not an addition to the rights. It is the condition under which interdependence produces autonomy rather than consuming it. *If* autonomy is to last.

This also explains section 3. Deliberate manipulation, by words or by devices, is harmful in a particular way. It uses a connection, which should feed the other's autonomy, to take that autonomy over.

## 7. Why the bridge matters now

The UN debate is about exactly this choice. The Human Rights Council Advisory Committee recorded two views ([A/HRC/57/61](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g24/133/28/pdf/g2413328.pdf), 2024, para. 63): new neurorights for grey areas, or "an extensive interpretation of existing core rights complemented by implementation standards, and not new rights". It found that "human rights provide an adequate and flexible principle-based framework" (para. 78(c)). In April 2025 the Council asked the Committee to draft "recommended guidelines for applying the existing human rights framework" to neurotechnology, for its sixty-fourth session, and to seek the views of all stakeholders ([A/HRC/RES/58/6](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4082164/files/A_HRC_RES_58_6-EN.pdf), paras. 2–3). UNESCO adopted its [Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology](https://unesco.org.uk/site/assets/files/27459/unesco_ethics-neurotechnology_a5_en_digital-double-pages_1.pdf) on 11 November 2025.

This note supports the second view, with a reason the debate does not yet give. Existing rights already cover neural intervention, *if* they are read as protecting the integrity of a world model rather than a particular organ. Under that reading neurotechnology is a new route to an old harm. The same reading covers lies, concealed steering and AI systems, so it does not single out the brain (critics call that singling out "neuro-exceptionalism").

## 8. What this adds, in short

1. **One criterion across routes.** Harm is judged by what happens to the model's capacity to track and correct, not by the route. *Proposed*, resting on parts that *follow*.
2. **Duties beside rights.** UDHR art. 29(1) already speaks of duties to the community. The relational duties make them concrete. Deliberate deception is the plainest breach of the first; an honest error is no breach, and is what the duties to stay correctable and to attempt repair are for. *If* relationships are to last (relational-duties note).
3. **The collective level.** The integrity of shared world models is a commons: trust and the capacity to check are what run down. A deliberate deceiver gains while the commons loses, which is cost externalization, the mechanism of capture (D27). Distributed policing that stays correctable is how a commons tames it (`TamedCapture`). *If* the commons is to last.
4. **Across substrates.** The same duties hold between people and AI systems in both directions: a system that flatters or misleads degrades a person's model; a person who feeds a system poisoned data degrades its model. *Follows* for the mechanism; the asymmetry of position stays (relational-duties note, section 4).

## 9. Where the assistant is unsure

- **Thresholds and knowledge.** Human rights law binds states and targets grave cases. Whether something is a lie, and whether it was meant to steer, is often unknown, also to the speaker. Most falsehoods are matters of ethics and of duties of effort, not of courts. The boundary in section 5 is for deliberate manipulation.
- **The route still matters for remedy.** The outcome may be the same, but deception can often be found out through the person's own routes, while a neural intervention or a hidden shared layer may leave none. By `last_route_removal_seals`, influence that leaves no route to discovery deserves the strongest protection. So the criterion keeps the outcome in view and does not make every route equal.
- **"Better" by whose measure.** A model works better relative to its holder's own aims and evidence. Otherwise "improving someone's model" becomes paternalism, and an enabling environment becomes control. The Special Rapporteur warns that states could use that duty to justify "authoritarian control over channels of communication and information" (A/76/380, para. 41). The repository's answer is the interval: correction needs links, but links that force conformity stop correcting (`full_conformity_cannot_correct`). Policing must stay correctable (D27).
- **Not everything that misinforms is wrong.** Fiction, play, tact and comfort change models with consent or by convention. The consent criterion has to carry that, and it may not be enough on its own.
- **Value, not theorem.** That the integrity of world models should be a matter of rights at all is *proposed*. Persistence is not goodness (D7).
- **One conversation, one model family.** Human readers, lawyers and other model families should check it (working agreement 7).

## 10. Open questions

- **A contribution to the UN guidelines.** Resolution 58/6 asks for stakeholder views. A short submission could propose the functional criterion and the collective row of the table. Whether and how is the author's call.
- **Measurement.** How the integrity of a shared world model could be measured without the measure becoming a weapon. Domain C of the [health of intelligence](../health-of-intelligence/HEALTH_OF_INTELLIGENCE.md), correction and source independence, is the nearest instrument.
- **Formalization.** Deception as an unfaithful channel, held unfaithful on purpose, may already sit close to `FaithfulChannel` and `hidden_versus_revealed_conflict` in [Anchored Correctability](../anchored-correctability/README.md). Not yet attempted.
- **Related work not yet read for this note:** the literature on mental integrity and cognitive liberty (Ienca and Andorno; Bublitz), predictive processing and the transparency of self-models (Metzinger), and epistemic rights and epistemic injustice. To be checked in their sources before any claim rests on them.
