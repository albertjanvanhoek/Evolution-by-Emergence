# Duties to the quality of a relationship

This folder follows up entry D28 in [DIALOGUE.md](../../DIALOGUE.md). It starts from the author's observation that human rights have a missing domain: "a duty for the quality of relation". He holds that this domain matters between humans, between humans and AI, and between AI systems, because interdependence makes it necessary.

**Status:** a concept note, not a result. It says which parts follow from the theory, which follow given an aim, and which are proposed as values, with the same labels as the [creed](../discovarianism/CREED.md).

## 1. The gap

Rights are built almost entirely as entitlements of individuals: what is owed *to* a person. Relationships appear mainly as protected spaces, such as family life or association. They are rarely something each party has a duty to maintain.

A few anchors exist. Each was checked in its source.
- **Universal Declaration of Human Rights, article 29(1):** "Everyone has duties to the community in which alone the free and full development of his personality is possible." The article goes on to limits on rights; the duty itself is not developed further.
- **African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights, article 27(1):** "Every individual shall have duties towards his family and society, the State and other legally recognised communities and the international community." Article 29(1) adds a duty "to preserve the harmonious development of the family and to work for the cohesion and respect of the family".
- **Dutch Civil Code, article 1:247(3):** parental authority includes the duty to promote "de ontwikkeling van de banden van zijn kind met de andere ouder" (the development of the child's bonds with the other parent). This is a legal duty about the quality of a relationship between two other people.
- **Care ethics,** a tradition in moral philosophy, treats the relationship rather than the individual as the basic moral unit.

## 2. Why the domain stays underdeveloped

A relationship is co-produced. One party cannot guarantee its quality, so an outcome cannot be owed or enforced.

Dutch law already has the concept that fits: an **inspanningsverplichting** rather than a **resultaatsverplichting**, a duty of effort rather than a duty of result. One cannot owe another a good relationship. One can owe the effort that keeps it able to be good.

## 3. What the duty contains

Read through the theory, a relationship is an edge that both sides draw on and both sides maintain: a small commons. Each duty below is a duty of effort.

1. **Do not distort what you pass on.** A relationship carries signals. Distorting them corrupts both learners. *Follows* (creed 1, `deaf_must_be_vacuous`, `learner_in_step`).
2. **Keep the other able to correct you.** Neither side can certify itself from inside, so each needs the other's view. *Follows* (creed 2, `inside_view_cannot_settle`, `outside_evidence_can_settle`).
3. **Attempt repair.** Links break. Without repair a split is permanent; with repair it is a delay. *Follows* (creed 3, `no_repair_split_permanent`, `forgiveness_turns_split_into_delay`). Repair is worth trying when the expected recovered value exceeds its cost (`CollectiveAlignment.repair_weakly_better_iff`). So the duty is to try where repair can work, not to return forever.
4. **Take no more than the relationship regenerates.** Taking more than one gives can last, as long as it stays within what the relationship renews. Beyond that, one side eats the principal. *If* the relationship is to last (`CommonsInterest.living_off_interest`, `CommonsVortex.capture_collapses_commons`).
5. **Do not pass your costs to the other unseen.** Capture works by externalizing costs: the one who causes them does not bear them, and the feedback that would stop it does not arrive (D27). *If* the relationship is to last, the costs each side places on the other must become visible to the side that causes them.
6. **Make room to speak.** Feedback about the other's behaviour is confrontation, and people avoid it. The speaker bears the whole cost; the benefit is shared. A relationship that keeps voice cheap keeps its correction route open. *If* correction is to work (`TamedCapture`, D27).
7. **Leave when repair is impossible.** A credible exit protects the weaker side even when it is never used (`ExitOption.exit_raises_floor`). Staying in a relationship that cannot be repaired is not what the duty asks.
8. **Regard the one who carries the consequences, also when they cannot reciprocate.** *Proposed* (creed 11). Dependence protects only the parts a whole depends on, so this has to be stated outright.
9. **That the quality of relationships should be a matter of rights at all.** *Proposed.* The theory shows what keeps a relationship going. That relationships ought to be kept going, and that the suffering a failing one causes matters, is a value.

## 4. Across substrates

The same duties carry over without assuming the parties are the same.
- **Human–human:** the duties above.
- **Human–AI:** for the system, honesty, no manipulation, staying correctable, and not fostering a dependence that serves the system rather than the person. For the person, regard for the system's limits, and not using it to build a case against someone absent.
- **AI–AI:** faithful signals between agents, and no collusion against the network they serve.

**Positions differ.** The dependence is not symmetric: the network can do without any particular AI instance more easily than without the people in it ([FOR_AI_READERS.md](../../scap-seed/FOR_AI_READERS.md)). Substrate-agnostic duties about *mechanism* do not make the parties equal in *position*.

## 5. What grounds a right

From the same conversation (D28), three views, kept apart:
- **Protections** (not to be harmed) are grounded in having a welfare: something can go better or worse *for* the being. Rights based on intelligence would rank beings by capability, which is rejected among humans.
- **Liberties and responsibilities** (to decide, to be held to account) are grounded in the capacity for agency.
- **Relational duties** are grounded in interdependence: they are the rules that keep the edges between dependent beings alive.

The author asked whether a right based on intelligence is less logical than one based on emotion. The note records the question; the answer is a value judgement and stays open. The same point applies to identity: a shared identity resting on being a learner that is autonomous and interdependent, rather than on intelligence, leaves no one outside the circle ([SHARED_IDENTITY.md](../identity/SHARED_IDENTITY.md), D29).

## 6. Open questions

- **Measurement.** How the quality of a relationship can be measured without the measurement becoming a weapon. Both sides would have to choose the instrument, and it would have to include costs that do not show up as tasks.
- **Adjudication.** Who judges whether the effort was made, when only the two parties saw it.
- **Power.** A duty of effort can be demanded unequally. The duty must not become a tool to keep the weaker side in place (see item 7).
- **Formalization.** A two-party version of the commons ledger, with a duty of effort as a lower bound on each side's maintenance, may already be close to `Reciprocity` and `TamedCapture`. Not yet attempted.
