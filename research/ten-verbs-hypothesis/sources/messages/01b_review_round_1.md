# Adversarial review: The Ten Verbs Hypothesis

**Bottom line:** The framing is careful about reality, the commons and individual models, and it avoids the obvious traps: it doesn't equate the commons with consensus or truth, and it doesn't claim disagreement is harmful. But the hypothesis itself is hedged so heavily that it is close to unfalsifiable. It quietly combines three outcomes that can pull in opposite directions. And its main foundational assumption fails in exactly the area where relationships happen. Most serious problems first:

## 1. Near-circular core claim

The verbs are defined as the mechanisms of correction ("observation, testing, honest representation, updating…"). The hypothesis then predicts that keeping them working supports correction. Unless each verb can be measured without reference to the outcome, the claim reduces to "learning relationships keep learning when learning keeps working."

The hedges make this worse. You have "depend *partly*," "*increases the risk*," "*proposed* protective factor," "*conditional* relationship." The scope section adds "not unique, not universally necessary." Read together, it's hard to name any result that would embarrass the hypothesis. A relationship with impaired verbs that thrives is "partly" or "conditional." One with intact verbs that fails had other factors.

**Fix:** Add a section called "What would count against it," as you did in the *Present Persistence* working note. Name at least one observable pattern the hypothesis forbids.

## 2. Truth-tracking and relationship persistence can trade off

The text treats three outcomes as if they move together:
- individual model quality
- quality of the commons
- persistence of cooperative relationships

They often don't. Long marriages survive on agreed avoidance and tact. Groups bond through shared beliefs that are false or untestable, and they last for centuries. Your own EbE notes treat religious narratives as second-order evolution that keeps relationships alive. Work on identity-protective cognition (Kahan) and on group cohesion shows that correcting someone can *cost* the relationship.

A critic will say: "Your own framework predicts that cohesion mechanisms that don't depend on correction exist and work. So corrective capacity is one route to persistence among several, and sometimes it competes with the others." The hypothesis has to say when correction protects a relationship and when it threatens one. Otherwise its strongest counterexamples come from the author's own body of work.

## 3. "Reality doesn't depend on representations" fails for social reality

R is defined as independent of whether representations agree. But money, law, trust, reputation and the relationship itself are partly *made of* shared representations (Searle's social facts, performativity, self-fulfilling prophecies). You have called EbE itself "self-fulfilling." The disagreements that threaten cooperation are mostly about social facts and values, not about physics. So the realist assumption fits the domain the hypothesis cares least about.

**Fix:** Say explicitly that C partly constitutes some regions of R, and explain what correction means there.

## 4. The relational hypothesis excludes most real disagreements

"Incompatible propositions cannot all be true under the same definitions and conditions" is just the law of non-contradiction. The qualifier "same definitions and conditions" takes out definitional disputes. Real conflicts are also often about:
- **Values:** two flawless learners can disagree about what matters, and correction doesn't apply.
- **Priors:** Aumann-style agreement needs common priors. Without them, perfect updaters can rationally stay apart.
- **Interests:** a party may gain from an unresolved disagreement. The verbs assume both sides want to be corrected.

What's left is factual disagreement between cooperative parties with commensurable priors. That's a narrow and fairly easy case.

## 5. Policing is missing

The verbs are all cooperative. Nothing covers sanctioning, refusing, setting boundaries or excluding bad-faith actors. That matters because:
- **Repair** is exploitable. Unconditional forgiveness gets farmed by defectors.
- **Share** and **Be honest** aren't incentive-compatible in adversarial settings.

You hold elsewhere that distributed policing is essential, and the creed has its sins. The hypothesis drops all of this. As written, it describes learning among trustworthy parties and says nothing about keeping the commons robust against capture. That's the problem you care most about.

## 6. "Share" is not monotonic, and the collective claim is not new

Zollman's network-epistemology results show that *more* connectivity and information-sharing can lock a community into a wrong consensus faster. Some temporary isolation, or "insulation from criticism," protects useful diversity. Kuhn made a related point about normal science.

So the collective prediction, that a commons open to criticism resists shared error better, is true only in a range. It isn't true monotonically. The non-monotonic part is also the only part that would be new. Without it, the claim restates Popper's open society, Merton's organized skepticism and communalism, and especially Longino's conditions for social objectivity (venues for criticism, uptake, shared standards, tempered equality). "Epistemic commons" also has prior art in Hess and Ostrom's *Understanding Knowledge as a Commons*. A reviewer will ask what this adds. A good answer: substrate-agnosticism plus a measurable claim about where the optimum lies.

## 7. Internal inconsistencies

- **Nine mechanisms, ten verbs.** The central hypothesis lists nine mechanisms: observation, testing, honest representation, updating, memory, resource preservation, information exchange, relational repair, methodological development. **Stay open** has no matching mechanism. Either name its function (receptivity to disconfirming input, perhaps) or justify it as a meta-condition of the other nine.
- **The labels are imperatives in a descriptive claim.** "Be honest," "Take care" and "Stay open" are creed language. The hypothesis is functional. The moral weight of the words gives the reader a sense of plausibility that the argument hasn't earned. Concrete mismatches:
  - "Take care" for *resource preservation* will be read as caring for others.
  - "Be honest" blends honest reporting to others with accurate self-representation (self-deception), which are different failure modes.
- **No Forget.** Remember is in, but pruning, unlearning and letting go of outdated commitments are central to learning, both in biological memory and in a commons full of retracted findings. A ledger that only grows is a failure mode.
- **"Ten" is in the title** while the scope section disowns any claim that the decomposition is unique or necessary. The audit already found a counterexample to the necessity claim. The name promises more than the text defends.

## 8. Measurement problem

"Resistance to persistent shared error" needs a benchmark. R is assumed inaccessible to any one intelligence, so error can only be identified after the fact, using a later commons. That makes the collective prediction testable mainly in simulations, or in domains with ground truth that arrives later (forecasting, replication outcomes). Say so, and choose those domains.

## What would make it strong

1. **Rename the core claim to match what it defends,** for example "Corrective Capacity Hypothesis." Present the ten verbs as one proposed way to measure it. You can then test whether ten factors fit data better than three or four.
2. **State falsifiers.** Examples: verb-impaired dyads persisting and cooperating as well as intact ones on consequential tasks; knocking out a verb having no effect on correction rates.
3. **Add the tradeoff explicitly.** Predict when correction strengthens a relationship and when it strains it.
4. **Add a policing or boundary function, or explain why it falls outside the scope.**
5. **Testbeds:** agent-based knockouts of single verbs, including Zollman-type network sweeps; multi-agent LLM experiments; observational data with built-in correction traces (Wikipedia talk pages and reverts, open-source review threads, long-running scientific collaborations). Infectieradar-style cohort designs would also work for human dyads.

Item 2 is the most important single fix. Right now the text protects itself so well that nothing can refute it, and a hypothesis that can't be refuted can't be confirmed either.
