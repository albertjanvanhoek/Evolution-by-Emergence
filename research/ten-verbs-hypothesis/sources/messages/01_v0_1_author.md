please do an adversial review: The Ten Verbs Hypothesis
Reality, the epistemic commons, and individual world models
Status: Theoretical hypothesis informed by social epistemology and Evolution by Emergence. Its relational predictions require empirical testing.
Foundational assumptions
Reality (R) is the shared world in which interacting intelligences exist and act. Its existence and constraints do not depend on whether their representations agree. No intelligence is assumed to possess a complete representation of it.
The epistemic commons (C) is the collectively accessible and maintained body of representations, evidence, methods, records, concepts, and interpretations concerning reality. It develops through interactions between intelligences and can extend beyond what any individual knows.
The commons is fallible. It may contain competing theories, contradictory propositions, unresolved questions, and mistaken beliefs. It must not be equated with reality or reduced to consensus.
Individual world models (Mᵢ) are the representations of reality maintained by individual intelligences. They are informed by personal observations, prior learning, and contributions from the epistemic commons.
Individual models may agree with, differ from, or contradict portions of the commons and one another.
These are distinct epistemic roles, not three physically separate realms. The intelligences, their records, and their collective practices are themselves parts of reality.
Central hypothesis
The Ten Verbs Hypothesis proposes that the persistence and productive development of learning relationships depend partly on maintaining effective mechanisms of observation, testing, honest representation, updating, memory, resource preservation, information exchange, relational repair, and methodological development.
These mechanisms are represented by ten functional verbs:
Stay open, Try, Notice, Be honest, Change, Remember, Take care, Share, Repair, and Find new ways.
Through these functions, individual intelligences can correct their own models, contribute to the epistemic commons, and obtain corrections from other intelligences.
Relational hypothesis
When two intelligences maintain logically incompatible propositions about a shared reality, these propositions cannot all be true under the same definitions and conditions.
Such incompatibility does not establish which intelligence is mistaken.
The hypothesis predicts that persistent impairment of the learning and correction mechanisms increases the risk that consequential disagreements remain unresolved and interfere with cooperation, epistemic trust, and relationship maintenance.
Conversely, maintaining these mechanisms enables intelligences to identify contradictions, investigate competing claims, recognize uncertainty, and coordinate despite unresolved disagreements.
The relationship need not produce identical world models. Its continued capacity for learning and correction is the proposed protective factor.
Collective hypothesis
The epistemic commons benefits from individual contributions and independent correction. However, collective agreement does not guarantee accuracy.
The hypothesis predicts that a commons capable of retaining competing interpretations, evaluating evidence, incorporating justified corrections, and developing new methods will be more resistant to persistent shared errors than one in which prevailing interpretations are insulated from criticism.
Scope
The ten verbs are proposed functional categories rather than a proven unique or universally necessary decomposition of learning.
The hypothesis does not assume that disagreement is harmful in itself, that all disagreements can be resolved, or that productive learning guarantees ethical conduct.
It proposes a conditional relationship between corrective capacity, the quality of individual and collective world models, and the persistence of cooperative relationships.
