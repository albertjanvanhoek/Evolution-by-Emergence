I agree with the main criticism in round 4: **the Ten Verbs Hypothesis now needs to compete with existing explanations of learning, not merely demonstrate that its ten named functions can be impaired.**

But I would correct two points in the review.

First, *Be honest, Share,* and *Repair* are not scientifically unexplored functions. Honesty and deception have substantial literatures in signalling and communication games; social information transmission has been modelled computationally; and interpersonal trust repair is an established research field.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.sciencedirect.com\&sz=32)

sciencedirect.com+2



The potential novelty is therefore not those three behaviours individually. It is whether **the same functional framework explains error correction within an intelligence and across relationships between intelligences**, including the effects of disrupted communication and misleading information.

Second, failure to recover ten parameters in a simulation would show that the particular experimental design cannot distinguish them. It would not, by itself, disprove the theory. But if the functions remain indistinguishable across well-designed tasks, and simpler models predict behaviour equally well, the proposed ten-way decomposition becomes scientifically difficult to defend.

That is the risk I think we should accept.

## **Three different scientific claims**

I suggest we stop making the number ten carry all the philosophical weight.

| Claim                                                                    | Scientific status                                     | What could refute it?                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. Learning is a correctable process**                                 | General theoretical foundation; substantial prior art | Not the main novel claim                                                                              |
| **B. Ten distinguishable functional roles explain correction**           | Primary Ten Verbs Hypothesis                          | Failure of functional separation or predictive improvement over simpler models                        |
| **C. Failures of these functions explain certain relational breakdowns** | Relational extension                                  | No independent effect on error resolution or cooperation once established explanations are considered |

This preserves the theory's origin in the problem of incompatible world models without requiring us to prove that all ten functions are universally necessary.

## **The Ten Verbs Hypothesis — version 0.5**

# The Ten Verbs Hypothesis

### Functional differentiation of learning and correction between intelligences

**Status:** Proposed hypothesis, awaiting model specification, identifiability testing, and empirical validation.

### 1. Foundation

Interacting intelligences operate within a shared reality while maintaining incomplete, potentially incompatible individual world models.

Through communication and external records, they participate in multiple, overlapping epistemic commons. These commons exist between individual intelligences, can persist in external media, and may contain mutually contradictory claims.

There are three overlapping ways to identify problems in a world model: encounters with reality, examination of one's own reasoning, and information received from other intelligences.

Each can reveal errors. Each can also propagate or preserve errors.

### 2. Primary hypothesis

**The Ten Verbs Hypothesis proposes that learning and correction involve ten functionally distinguishable operations whose contributions can be identified by their characteristic effects across checking processes and environmental conditions.**

The hypothesis predicts that these operations collectively explain error detection, belief revision, and corrective interaction better than simpler computational models of learning.

The ten operations need not be causally independent. Some may constitute stages of a learning process, others conditions that sustain it, and others mechanisms of interaction between intelligences.

Their functional distinctions must nevertheless yield additional predictive information beyond simpler models.

### 3. Two dimensions of differentiation

The hypothesis predicts that some functions primarily differ by their role in reality-mediated, reasoning-mediated, or commons-mediated checking.

Others become distinguishable through the conditions under which learning takes place.

The following matrix states the proposed differences.

| Verb                  | Primary checking process        | Condition exposing its distinctive contribution                               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Stay open**         | Reasoning and commons           | Disconfirming evidence versus confirming evidence                             |
| **Try**               | Reality                         | Tests available for selection versus observations imposed externally          |
| **Notice**            | Reality                         | Noisy perception versus externally supplied accurate records                  |
| **Be honest**         | Commons, and internal recording | Private-to-public reporting with versus without incentives to misrepresent    |
| **Change**            | All three                       | Contradictory evidence detected but not yet incorporated                      |
| **Remember / let go** | Across all checking processes   | Stable versus changing environments                                           |
| **Take care**         | Sustains all three              | Scarce versus plentiful learning resources                                    |
| **Share**             | Commons                         | Asymmetric private information versus information already mutually accessible |
| **Repair**            | Commons                         | Previously disrupted versus continuously functioning correction channels      |
| **Find new ways**     | Reality, reasoning and commons  | Existing methods unable versus able to distinguish competing explanations     |

Each row predicts a directional interaction: impairment should have a greater adverse effect on the relevant process measure in the first condition than in the contrasting condition, except where the order is explicitly reversed by the described resource or information availability.

The predictions concern selective contrasts, not necessarily improved overall accuracy in every condition.

### 4. Competing models

The hypothesis will be tested against four prespecified candidate models.

**M1 — General learning capacity.** A single latent factor accounts for differences in error detection, learning and correction.

**M2 — Established computational learning model.** A reinforcement-learning or hierarchical Bayesian model represents uncertainty, information seeking, updating, forgetting, and social information weighting. The exact model structure and parameterization must be fixed before comparison.

**M3 — Three-component model.** Learning is represented by a core individual learning process, a social exchange and correction process, and a maintenance and methodological adaptation process.

**M4 — Ten Verbs model.** Ten explicitly specified functional components govern learning and correction, including their context-dependent interactions.

M4 must demonstrate superior predictive performance on held-out observations and intervention conditions, accounting for differences in model complexity.

Its greater number of parameters is not itself evidence for ten distinguishable functions.

### 5. Identifiability

Before empirical application, the candidate models will undergo parameter-recovery and model-recovery simulations.

Synthetic observations generated under known parameters will be used to establish whether the parameters can be separately recovered.

Synthetic observations generated by each competing model will also be fitted using the other models, establishing whether the experimental design can discriminate among them.

Failure of recovery within a particular design indicates inadequate identifiability. Persistent failure across well-designed conditions, or absence of predictive gains over simpler models, weakens the proposed decomposition.

Recovery of parameters from data generated by M4 is necessary for interpreting its parameter estimates, but does not establish that M4 correctly describes real intelligences.

### 6. Relational hypothesis

**When two intelligences hold incompatible factual models of consequential shared events, failures in truthful communication, information exchange, and restoration of correction pathways will increase the persistence of unresolved errors and reduce effective coordination, relative to comparable interactions in which these functions remain available.**

The proposed mechanism is obstruction of correction, not disagreement itself.

A wider epistemic commons can provide additional evidence, alternative methods, or independent criticism. It can also reproduce and amplify errors.

The effect of commons-mediated correction is therefore predicted to depend on evidence quality, provenance, source dependence, and the receiving intelligence's ability to evaluate the information.

Epistemic accuracy, mutual understanding, cooperation, trust, and relationship persistence remain separate outcomes.

Correction may improve one while worsening another.

### 7. Contamination and boundaries

In environments containing misleading or strategically deceptive sources, the hypothesis predicts an interaction between source reliability and conditional sharing or repair.

Procedures using independent verification, source provenance, and evidence-based restrictions on access are predicted to reduce acceptance of false corrections compared with indiscriminate sharing and unconditional restoration of disrupted channels.

In environments where all available sources are accurate and cooperative, the same restrictions may delay useful correction.

These comparisons concern prespecified procedures, not practices defined retrospectively as successful.

### 8. Falsification

The distinctive ten-component hypothesis will be weakened or rejected if adequately discriminating experiments consistently show that simpler models reproduce the proposed functional signatures and predict held-out responses as well as or better than M4.

The relational hypothesis will be weakened if independently measured failures of corrective interaction do not explain subsequent unresolved factual errors or coordination difficulties beyond initial disagreement, information availability, incentive structure, and other prespecified competing explanations.

The hypothesis must allow individual learning to improve while relationships deteriorate, and relationships to persist while erroneous beliefs remain uncorrected.

**The claim being tested is not that ten virtues are desirable. It is that ten identifiable functions form a useful causal explanation of learning and correction, including correction between intelligences that do not share the same world model.**

## **Why parameter recovery should come first**

I agree with the recommendation to begin with simulation rather than human participants.

Wilson and Collins' [Ten simple rules for the computational modeling of behavioral data](https://elifesciences.org/articles/49547) (2019) is particularly relevant. 2\. Model recovery

Can an experiment distinguish M4 from simpler generating models rather than merely fit M4 to itself?

Rows: generating model. Columns: model selected by the fitting procedure. The actual experiment may produce substantial confusion.

One thing the simulation must not do is build the correct answer into its experimental conditions. If a task makes it impossible to obtain information without the *Share* operation, demonstrating that disabling Share prevents information transfer is not a discovery. It is a test that the software behaves as designed.

The scientifically interesting outcome is whether behaviour arising from a variety of generative mechanisms can be explained better by the proposed ten functions than by established alternatives.

## **My final adversarial concern about version 0.5**

I think the reviewer is right that the most interesting territory lies between intelligences. But I would not simply relocate novelty to honesty, sharing and repair.

A stronger possibility is that **the same learning functions recur when the object of learning changes**.

An intelligence learns about its environment. It learns about the reliability of its own methods. It learns about another intelligence's understanding. And, through the epistemic commons, it may learn about the collective processes through which information is generated and corrected.

This is a form of recursive learning, and it is closely related to the scale-independent logic you've been developing in EbE.

That could eventually produce a stronger hypothesis than a ten-parameter behavioural model: the same correction structure might recur across different levels of organization, while the operations needed to implement it vary.

But I wouldn't add that claim to version 0.5 yet. We should first test whether the proposed distinctions can be recovered and whether they genuinely improve predictions.

**My recommendation is to freeze version 0.5 as a candidate hypothesis and make identifiability and model recovery the next adversarial tests.** If the ten-way decomposition survives, it earns a stronger scientific claim. If it fails, the results should tell us whether the theory needs fewer functions, different boundaries, or a more explicitly hierarchical structure.

Either way, we learn something about the hypothesis rather than merely finding better language to defend it.
