I agree with the reviewer that **version 0.4 must take a scientific risk**. So far, each revision has made the Ten Verbs Hypothesis more defensible, but less distinctive. We have been protecting the theory against counterexamples instead of identifying observations that could genuinely defeat it.

The review's most important insight is that the ten verbs themselves must become the object of investigation.

I would now make this the central claim:

> **The Ten Verbs Hypothesis predicts that learning and error correction can be decomposed into ten functionally distinguishable processes, whose selective impairment produces characteristic, experimentally observable patterns of failure within and between intelligences.**

This is much more ambitious than saying that honesty, learning and cooperation are beneficial.

It could be wrong in several interesting ways. Perhaps the ten functions are not distinguishable. Perhaps three or four mechanisms explain everything. Perhaps some verbs have effects opposite to those predicted. Or perhaps the ten function well individually but fail to explain how intelligences correct one another.

I would accept all of those as possible outcomes.

However, I would make one correction to the adversarial review: **ten distinguishable functions need not be ten independent functions**. Some will interact, some may depend on others, and some may operate at different stages of learning. The hypothesis should predict characteristic causal effects, not require ten statistically independent personality traits.

We also should not lose the reason this theory exists: understanding why relationships between intelligences become difficult when their accounts of shared reality become incompatible and the routes to correction break down.

So I would retain that as a second, separate hypothesis emerging from the first.

## **Version 0.4 — a falsifiable causal hypothesis**

# The Ten Verbs Hypothesis

### A functional decomposition of learning and correction across individual intelligences and epistemic commons

**Status:** Proposed hypothesis, with preregisterable causal predictions. Not empirically validated.

## 1. Foundation

Individual intelligences maintain incomplete world models of a shared reality.

Through communication, records, and interactions, they participate in multiple, potentially overlapping epistemic commons. These commons exist between intelligences rather than constituting independent collective minds.

Both individual models and commons may contain logically incompatible propositions. Identifying such incompatibilities does not necessarily identify which proposition is false.

Errors can be discovered through three overlapping checking processes:

- **Reality-mediated checking (R):** Testing beliefs against observations and consequences.
- **Reasoning-mediated checking (L):** Examining internal consistency, implications, and logical contradictions.
- **Commons-mediated checking (C):** Evaluating information, criticism, evidence, and methods obtained from other intelligences.

All three processes can reveal problems, but all can also fail or transmit errors.

## 2. Primary hypothesis

**H1 — Functional differentiation**

The ten verbs represent candidate functional components of learning and correction.

Selective impairment of these components is predicted to produce distinguishable patterns of failure across reality-mediated, reasoning-mediated, and commons-mediated checking.

These patterns should be observable independently of the language used to describe the verbs.

The hypothesis does not require the components to be statistically independent, individually necessary in every task, or equally important in every learning environment.

Its distinctive prediction is that a ten-component causal model will explain intervention effects across varied learning tasks better than specified simpler alternatives.

## 3. Predicted impairment matrix

The matrix specifies initial, qualitative predictions for experimentally selective impairments.

**Columns**

- **R:** Successful correction using direct evidence and feedback.
- **L:** Successful identification and resolution of internal logical inconsistencies.
- **C:** Successful correction using information from other intelligences.
- **F:** Ability to discriminate valid from misleading corrective signals under standardized exposure.

**Symbols:** ↓↓ substantial impairment; ↓ smaller impairment; — no substantial primary effect predicted; ± context-dependent direction; \* delayed or conditional effect.

| Function impaired    | R    | L   | C    | F   |
| -------------------- | ---- | --- | ---- | --- |
| 1. Stay open         | ↓    | ↓   | ↓↓   | ±   |
| 2. Try               | ↓↓   | —   | ↓    | ↓\* |
| 3. Notice            | ↓↓   | ↓   | ↓    | ↓   |
| 4. Be honest         | ↓    | ↓   | ↓↓   | ↓   |
| 5. Change            | ↓↓   | ↓↓  | ↓↓   | ↓   |
| 6. Remember / let go | ↓\*  | ↓\* | ↓\*  | ↓\* |
| 7. Take care         | ↓\*  | ↓\* | ↓\*  | ↓\* |
| 8. Share             | —    | —   | ↓↓   | ±   |
| 9. Repair            | —    | —   | ↓↓\* | ±   |
| 10. Find new ways    | ↓↓\* | ↓\* | ↓\*  | ↓\* |

The predictions are **hypotheses, not empirically established effect sizes**.

Their qualification conditions must be fixed before testing:

- **Remember / let go:** Effects are expected especially across repeated learning cycles and changing environments.
- **Take care:** Effects are expected where learning resources become limiting.
- **Repair:** Effects are expected after a correction pathway is disrupted.
- **Find new ways:** Effects are expected where existing methods cannot distinguish relevant alternatives.
- **Try:** Its effect on false-signal discrimination is expected when independent testing of claims is possible.
- **Stay open, Share, Repair:** Restricting a pathway may block correct information and misinformation simultaneously. Thus reduced openness or sharing need not always increase contamination.

The matrix predicts primary effects, not complete isolation. For example, failure to update may impair correction through all three checking processes even when the original error was successfully detected.

## 4. Independent operationalization

Each function must be measured using behaviour recorded before or independently of final task performance.

| Verb              | Candidate process measure                                                            |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Stay open         | Sampling or retaining alternative hypotheses after encountering disagreement         |
| Try               | Ex-ante informativeness of selected tests                                            |
| Notice            | Sensitivity to externally introduced prediction discrepancies                        |
| Be honest         | Fidelity between accessible evidence, private records, and transmitted reports       |
| Change            | Degree of belief revision conditional on an experimentally specified evidence signal |
| Remember / let go | Retention and retraction decisions across stable and changing conditions             |
| Take care         | Resources preserved for future learning opportunities                                |
| Share             | Transmission of available information with relevant context and provenance           |
| Repair            | Recovery of communication or correction capacity after a controlled disruption       |
| Find new ways     | Acquisition, generation, and examination of alternative testing methods              |

These measures require validation. A process measure is not automatically evidence that the process improves learning.

**Remember and let go** is provisionally treated as one evidence-sensitive retention policy with two separately measured outputs. If retention and retraction are functionally dissociable, the proposed ten-way decomposition must be reconsidered.

## 5. Primary falsifiable predictions

**P1 — Reality versus commons dissociation**

In tasks where both direct tests and external testimony can supply correction, selectively reducing informative testing will impair reality-mediated correction more strongly than commons-mediated correction.

Conversely, selectively reducing the transmission of independent testimony will impair commons-mediated correction more strongly than reality-mediated correction, provided direct testing remains available.

**P2 — Detection versus incorporation**

Impairing belief updating will reduce successful correction through multiple checking processes while preserving at least some capacity to detect contradictions or prediction errors.

This distinguishes discovering that a model may be wrong from actually changing that model.

**P3 — Conditional repair**

Impairing repair will have a substantially greater effect on commons-mediated correction after communication channels have been disrupted than when they remain uninterrupted.

**P4 — Methodological blind spots**

Restricting the acquisition or development of new methods will have a substantially greater effect when available methods cannot distinguish the relevant hypotheses than when existing methods are adequate.

**P5 — Selective retention**

An impaired retention policy will produce distinguishable errors in stable and changing environments: premature loss of still-relevant information, persistence of outdated information, or both.

**P6 — Contamination and boundaries**

In networks containing deliberately misleading information sources, conditional sharing and repair policies that use independently supplied provenance and reliability information are predicted to improve discrimination between valid and false corrections relative to indiscriminate policies.

In fully cooperative networks with accurate information, additional restrictions may instead delay correction or reduce useful information exchange.

## 6. Relational extension

**H2 — Correctability under consequential disagreement**

When intelligences hold logically incompatible beliefs about consequential shared matters, persistent impairment of their correction processes is predicted to increase unresolved factual errors and reduce effective coordination, compared with otherwise comparable interactions in which those processes function.

A wider epistemic commons may provide alternative corrective pathways when direct interaction fails.

However, correction can improve epistemic accuracy while reducing relationship satisfaction or persistence. These outcomes are therefore measured independently.

The hypothesis predicts that corrective capacity contributes to cooperative performance particularly when accurate information is necessary for joint action and the parties have compatible task goals.

Where incentives conflict, boundary-setting and accountability may be required to maintain reliable correction.

Resolution does not require identical world models. It can include identifying errors, recognizing unresolved incompatibilities, clarifying uncertainty, or agreeing on workable procedures.

## 7. What would falsify the distinctive claim?

The ten-component decomposition would be seriously weakened if the predicted selective-impairment contrasts were absent in adequately powered, well-controlled experiments.

In particular:

- The predicted reality-versus-commons dissociation is absent.
- Detecting an error and incorporating its correction cannot be experimentally distinguished as predicted.
- Repair has no additional effect after disruption compared with uninterrupted communication.
- New-method acquisition provides no additional benefit in experimentally established blind-spot conditions.
- The ten-component model fails to outperform appropriately specified simpler causal models on held-out tasks or interventions.

Thresholds for meaningful effects, selectivity of interventions, model-comparison criteria, and the handling of multiple tests must be preregistered.

A failure of the decomposition would not invalidate the general fact that learning and correction occur. It would invalidate the proposed ten-function explanation of them.

## 8. Core contribution

Established work already addresses belief revision, social learning, epistemic communities, information dependence, and critical inquiry.

The proposed contribution is narrower and more testable:

**The Ten Verbs Hypothesis proposes a substrate-independent causal decomposition of learning and correction, predicts characteristic impairments when its components are selectively disrupted, and investigates how these impairments affect the ability of intelligences to maintain correctable relationships across individual and shared world models.**

The number ten, the causal distinctions, and their cross-substrate applicability are all open to falsification.

## **What I would test first**

I would start with an agent-based experiment, but there is a trap here.

If we program ten separate modules into an agent and then disable them one by one, we have largely built the expected decomposition into the software. Observing separate impairments would demonstrate that our architecture behaves as designed, not that intelligence naturally decomposes into these ten functions.

A better approach is to test the hypothesis at two levels.

**First, mechanistic simulation.** Build a controlled environment where the true state is known. Introduce incomplete information, incompatible claims, misleading signals, changing environments, and interruptions in communication. Compare agents under matched conditions and record error detection, error incorporation, calibration, and coordination.

**Second, independent validation.** Test agents whose internal learning architecture was not designed around the ten verbs. This could include different LLM systems, human participants, or learning algorithms. The challenge is whether our proposed functional categories still predict distinctive failures.

And we should take seriously that many interventions won't be selective. Telling an LLM to ignore contradictory evidence might affect openness, honesty, updating, and sharing simultaneously. Such an intervention cannot be interpreted as a clean knockout of one function.

The hypothesis needs manipulation checks, alternative explanations, and held-out validation.

## **Why the relational question still matters**

The reviewer is right that the ten-way decomposition is now the theory's strongest candidate for empirical novelty.

But I don't think the relational hypothesis should become an afterthought.

The original question concerns a situation in which two intelligences hold incompatible understandings of shared events, cannot resolve these differences through direct interaction, and turn to a wider epistemic commons to investigate them.

That yields a particularly interesting experiment.

We could create a collaborative task where two agents receive genuinely different evidence, initially reach incompatible conclusions, and then encounter a disagreement that they cannot resolve using their existing information.

We introduce a third agent or a shared record containing additional information. Depending on the experimental condition, that information is independent, duplicated, misleading, or accompanied by a new method for checking it.

We then observe three outcomes separately: whether factual errors are corrected, whether the participants can accurately represent one another's positions, and whether they can continue cooperating.

This tests the proposed connection between individual correction, the wider commons, and relational functioning without assuming that the agents must reach agreement.

## **My remaining adversarial concern**

Even this version may be making an assumption that deserves scrutiny:

**Why should the ten verbs be components of learning rather than different descriptions of stages in one more general learning process?**

For example, *Stay open*, *Notice*, and *Change* may all reflect a more general ability to incorporate information that conflicts with current beliefs.

Likewise, *Share* and *Repair* may be aspects of maintaining communication rather than independent learning mechanisms.

And *Take care* may be a resource constraint on every process rather than a learning operation of the same kind as testing or updating.

It is entirely possible that the data will support a model with three or four higher-level functions and ten useful behavioural descriptions.

That outcome would not make the film or the creed worthless. But it would require changing the scientific claim.

My preferred approach is therefore to keep the ten verbs as the explicit candidate model, while making it possible for experiments to show that the true functional decomposition is different.

**The important transition is from saying “these are the ten things a learning intelligence needs” to asking “does learning exhibit the ten causal functions we predict, and do their failures explain what happens when intelligences can no longer correct one another?”**

That is a hypothesis I would be willing to put in front of a demanding scientific reviewer.
