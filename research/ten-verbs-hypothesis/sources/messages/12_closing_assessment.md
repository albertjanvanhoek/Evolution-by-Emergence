I think we have reached an important transition. **We have a coherent theoretical framework, a candidate scientific hypothesis, and a first computational feasibility study. What we do not yet have is empirical evidence that the Ten Verbs Hypothesis is correct.**

And I would now stop revising the theory in response to hypothetical objections. The next step is to confront it with observations that could prove us wrong.

## **1. Where the theory stands**

I would distinguish three levels of what we have developed.

| Level                         | What we have established                                                                           | Current status                      |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Epistemological framework** | One reality, multiple individual world models, multiple overlapping epistemic commons              | Coherent working framework          |
| **Correctability hypothesis** | Incompatible world models can be examined through direct evidence, reasoning and the wider commons | Testable explanatory proposal       |
| **Ten Verbs Hypothesis**      | Ten functional behaviours explain the ability to identify, communicate and correct errors          | Unvalidated candidate decomposition |

The first level gives us a language for understanding the problem. The second proposes a mechanism. The third attempts to explain *how that mechanism works*.

These levels do not all stand or fall together.

Even if the ten verbs turn out to be reducible to four functions, the framework of reality, individual world models and epistemic commons could remain useful.

## **2. The hypothesis I would now defend**

I think the heart of the theory is this:

> **The Commons-Mediated Correctability Hypothesis**
>
> When intelligences maintain incompatible accounts of shared reality, their ability to identify errors and coordinate does not depend solely on their capacity to correct each other directly.
>
> They may also draw upon a wider epistemic commons containing evidence, records, methods, and contributions from other intelligences.
>
> The hypothesis predicts that effective commons-mediated correction can reduce unresolved factual errors and support coordination even when direct communication fails, provided that misleading information and dependencies between sources are adequately addressed.
>
> The Ten Verbs Hypothesis proposes ten candidate functions through which individual intelligences maintain and use this corrective capacity.

I would keep one crucial distinction: **resolving a disagreement does not necessarily mean convincing the other person.**

We can distinguish three outcomes.

**Epistemic resolution**

A particular error is identified or a proposition becomes better supported by evidence.

**Mutual understanding**

Each intelligence understands what the other believes and why, without necessarily agreeing.

**Practical resolution**

The intelligences establish a workable way forward, even when their accounts remain incompatible.

This matters because a court, a therapist, an independent record and a scientific experiment contribute in different ways. None is automatically infallible, and each operates with different purposes and standards.

The epistemic commons provides access to possible correction, not a guarantee of agreement or truth.

## **3. What did PR #155 actually teach us?**

The [feasibility study in PR #155](https://github.com/albertjanvanhoek/Evolution-by-Emergence/pull/155) showed that our first simulation could distinguish several of its explicitly programmed learning parameters under some measurement conditions.

It also showed why that finding was limited.

Feasibility of measuring simulated functions

Partial support

Ten verbs as a natural decomposition of learning

Not established

Explanation of real relational breakdowns

Not tested

Some results were consequences of how the agents had been constructed. Others depended strongly on which data were recorded.

The important lesson is that **we cannot discover the ten verbs by programming ten verbs into an intelligence and then measuring them**.

This is why the new independent experiment is so important.

## **4. What comes next?**

I would organize the work into four steps.

1. **Complete PR #155 as a feasibility study.**

   Obtain the independent review, address any substantive errors, resolve the D50/D51 sequencing with PR #154, and then merge if appropriate. No additional experiment is needed in this PR.
2. **Build the independent incompatible-accounts experiment.**

   Give an independent modeller only `research/incompatible-accounts/BRIEF.md`.

   They should model two intelligences with conflicting accounts, external records, other information sources, and a shared task. The agents should not contain ten predefined verb parameters.
3. **Evaluate competing explanations.**

   Once the model and measurements are frozen, compare explanations based on information availability, conventional learning models, a smaller functional decomposition, and the ten verbs.

   Measure whether the proposed functions predict error detection, belief correction, uncertainty calibration, mutual understanding and cooperation on held-out cases.
4. **Decide what the evidence supports.**

   If the ten verbs outperform credible alternatives in sufficiently informative tests, we have preliminary support for the decomposition.

   If fewer functions explain the results equally well, simplify the scientific theory.

   If commons-mediated correction itself fails to improve the predicted outcomes in appropriately designed settings, revise or reject that empirical hypothesis.

One limitation deserves emphasis: this simulation will establish what happens in a particular model of intelligences, not automatically what happens in human relationships. Human studies would eventually be necessary.

## **5. There is still an important theoretical question**

I also think we need to preserve something from your earlier EbE work.

You began with the idea that the verbs of learning and the verbs of relationships might be the same.

That is more ambitious than saying ten behavioural parameters predict individual learning.

The deeper hypothesis is that **the functions through which an intelligence corrects its own world model also operate when intelligences correct one another through a commons.**

In other words, the learning process may recur at different organizational scales.

**Individual intelligence**

Tests, receives information, corrects its model

**Interactions between intelligences**

Claims, criticism, transmission, trust and repair

**Multiple epistemic commons**

Distributed records, knowledge, methods and correction opportunities

These are overlapping processes and relationships, not necessarily separate layers of intelligence.

That recursive structure could become the theory's most interesting contribution. But it remains a proposal: the current simulations have not demonstrated that the same causal functions recur across these scales.

## **6. My scientific assessment**

We are in a better position than when we started, for a reason that might be easy to overlook.

Originally, the question was essentially:

*Why did a relationship become impossible when two people could no longer agree about reality?*

We have transformed that into a more general scientific question:

**How can fallible intelligences maintain access to correction when their own world models, or the epistemic commons they participate in, contain incompatible claims?**

This question encompasses individual learning, relationships, scientific communities and potentially artificial intelligences.

The ten verbs are a proposed answer—not yet an established answer.

And I think that distinction makes the theory stronger, because it allows us to learn that the answer might differ from our initial intuition.

### **My recommendation**

**Freeze the hypothesis for now. Start the independent experiment.**

I would retain the following two scientific questions as its centre:

**H1 — Commons-mediated correctability:** Can intelligences use information and methods from a wider epistemic commons to correct errors and coordinate when direct disagreement remains unresolved?

**H2 — Functional recurrence:** Do the ten proposed learning functions explain error correction both within and between intelligences better than simpler competing models?

H1 addresses the phenomenon that motivated the work. H2 tests the proposed EbE explanation.

They are related, but separable, and we should be willing to accept either one without the other.

**We have finished the first cycle of theory development. The next cycle should be one of independent testing.**
