# Plan for the second set of experiments (written before running them)

**Status: fixed before any of these experiments was run** (committed on its own, ahead of the code). It answers the instructions in [../sources/NEXT_STEP_INSTRUCTIONS.md](../sources/NEXT_STEP_INSTRUCTIONS.md). Anything changed after the runs started will be listed in the results as a deviation.

All four models share the same agent engine as the first experiments ([model.py](model.py)): the same task, the same observable channels and the same functional forms. They differ only in how the ten verb settings are produced, and in two structural switches. So every model predicts exactly the same data: test decisions, test choices, outcomes passed on, link repairs, method acquisition and belief reports.

## 1. The four models

Each verb *v* was drawn in the first experiments from a range [lo, hi] (`PRIOR` in model.py). Write *x*ᵥ ∈ [0, 1] for its position in that range, so *v* = lo + *x*ᵥ (hi − lo).

| Model | Free parameters | How the ten settings are produced | Structural switches |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M4, ten verbs** | 10 | each *x*ᵥ free | Stay open weights only testimony that disagrees with the current best guess; Notice threshold on |
| **M2, a conventional learner** | 9 | free: learning rate (Change), forgetting toward uniform (Remember / let go), a weight on all testimony (*s*), directed exploration (Try); and the five behavioural rates Be honest, Share, Repair, Take care, Find new ways | testimony weighted symmetrically: exponent Change × *s* for agreeing and disagreeing testimony alike; no Notice (every own observation counts fully) |
| **M3, three layers** | 3 | individual layer *g*₁: Try, Notice, Change have *x* = *g*₁, Remember / let go has *x* = 1 − *g*₁; interpersonal layer *g*₂: Stay open, Be honest, Share, Repair have *x* = *g*₂; maintenance and method layer *g*₃: Take care, Find new ways have *x* = *g*₃ | as M4 |
| **M1, one general capacity** | 1 | every *x*ᵥ = *g*, except Remember / let go, *x* = 1 − *g* | as M4 |

Notes, fixed now:
- **M2 is a simple standard form** (tempered Bayesian updating with forgetting, a social weight and information-directed choice), **not** a hierarchical Gaussian filter or a reinforcement-learning model with value learning. It is the closest conventional learner this task allows. Its behavioural rates are free because a model must predict the same data as the others; they use the same forms as in M4.
- **The mappings in M1 and M3 are choices.** They are monotone, and they make higher capacity mean more retention (lower letting go). Other mappings are possible. A different mapping could change which model wins.
- **M4 has no added interactions.** It is the model of the first experiments, unchanged.

## 2. Fitting and comparison

- **Split:** each agent's 60 rounds are split into rounds 0–39 (fit) and 40–59 (held out). Beliefs are replayed through all rounds; only the likelihood terms of the held-out rounds are scored.
- **Primary comparison: held-out log-likelihood**, summed over all observable channels in rounds 40–59. The model with the highest value is "selected".
- **Secondary: BIC** on the fit to rounds 0–39.
- **Fitting:** maximum likelihood, L-BFGS-B, three random starts, as in the first experiments. For M4 and M2 the behavioural channels separate from the belief channels and are fitted in closed form; M1 and M3 are fitted jointly over all channels. The first experiments showed that three starts do not always find the best fit with sparse choices; this is kept as it was and reported, not fixed after the fact.

## 3. Experiment E5: model recovery across conditions

- **Generators** (all free parameters drawn uniformly on the unit scale):
  1. M1; 2. M2; 3. M3; 4. M4;
  5. **M4-distinct**: M4 settings drawn by rejection so that within the individual layer and within the interpersonal layer the verbs differ by at least 0.5 on the unit scale (behaviour a three-layer model should not be able to mimic);
  6. **M4-near-M3**: settings from the M3 mapping plus Gaussian noise (SD 0.05) on each *x*, clipped (behaviour near the boundary where M3 and M4 are almost equivalent).
- **Cells:** 7 conditions × 2 recording regimes (rich, sparse) × 6 generators; 20 agents per cell (4 groups of 5, all from the same generator).
- **Fitted:** each agent with M1, M2, M3 and M4.
- **Criterion, fixed now:** M4 counts as **recoverable** in a condition and regime if held-out selection picks M4 for at least 14 of the 20 agents generated by M4 (70%). This threshold is a convention of this plan, not a standard. Only in such cells does a preference for a simpler model mean anything about M4; elsewhere it is a property of the design.
- **Expectations, stated in advance (about the design, not the hypothesis):** M4 recoverable with rich reports in at least 4 of 7 conditions; with sparse choices in at most 2 of 7.

## 4. Experiment E6: too few observations, or not identifiable?

- Base condition, generator M4, **120 rounds instead of 60**, 100 agents, both regimes; refit as in E1.
- **Rule, fixed now:** a verb whose recovery correlation rises by at least 0.1 with 120 rounds is limited by the number of observations. A verb that does not rise and stays below 0.7 is limited by the structure of this design.
- **Expectation:** Find new ways is structural here, because each agent acquires the method once whatever the number of rounds.

## 5. Experiment E7: the Remember / let go crossover, replicated

**Note added after this plan was committed, before anything was run:** a second outside review ([../sources/REVIEW_OF_PR155_2.md](../sources/REVIEW_OF_PR155_2.md)) showed that the crossover is largely built into the model: the learner has no estimate of how often the world changes, so forgetting is its only way out of an outdated belief. E7 can therefore only test the model's consistency. The plan below is left as it was written.

**A replication, not an untouched prediction:** the crossover was seen in E4 at a switching rate of 0.06. Here: new seeds and new switching rates **0, 0.03 and 0.12** (other settings as E4's base condition and intact agents), 400 groups per cell, rounds 20–59, three measures (mean belief in the true state, Brier score, log score). Compared: no letting go (0) and letting go fast (0.3) against the intact setting (0.03).

Predictions, fixed now. "Beyond 3 SE" means the mean difference exceeds three standard errors.
- **P-a.** No letting go has a lower (better) Brier score than intact at switching rate 0, beyond 3 SE.
- **P-b.** No letting go has a higher (worse) Brier score than intact at 0.12, beyond 3 SE.
- **P-c.** No letting go has a worse log score than intact at 0.03 and at 0.12, beyond 3 SE.
- **P-d.** The Brier cost of letting go fast (0.3 against intact) is smaller at 0.12 than at 0.

All of this concerns this model. A confirmed crossover would show the model behaves consistently, not that learners have a Remember / let go function.

## 6. What will be reported

Every confusion matrix, every cell in which M4 is not recoverable, every case where a simpler model is selected on M4 data, and every prediction above that fails. No fitted parameter is read as evidence that a verb exists in real intelligences.
