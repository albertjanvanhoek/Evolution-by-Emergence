RESEARCH BRIEF: THE HEALTH OF THE COMMONS
Relational health and intelligence across persons, AI systems and the shared conditions they live in

Prepared for Albert Jan van Hoek, 7 October 2026

1. Background

Evolution by Emergence (EbE) is a theory of persistence in networks:
https://github.com/albertjanvanhoek/Evolution-by-Emergence
Read CORE.md and PREDICTIONS.md as the theory under assessment, not as evidence for anything.

An earlier round of research (two attached reports) looked for a shared, substrate-neutral
measure of the "health of intelligence" for humans, AI systems and institutions. Both reports
concluded:
- No validated cross-substrate instrument exists; the parts do (HUI3/15D cognition, WHO ICF,
  universal intelligence tests, AI calibration and continual learning, viability theory, Ostrom).
- Five candidate domains: grounding/calibration, adaptive learning with retention,
  correction with source independence, resource viability, robustness/recovery.
  Contribution to others is kept as a separate "network companion", not intrinsic health.
- Report a profile first; no product of ordinal scores; the score never weights a person's worth.
- Support should be reported in layers (standardized conditions, actual support, feasible
  improvement), never removing essential assistance to get an "individual" score.

Those reports treated the environment as context or as an outflow (contribution).
This brief asks about the missing piece: the shared conditions as an object with its own health.

2. The idea to investigate

A person's health and intelligence are relational. Examples:
- someone who cannot walk well but has a loving caretaker lives a very different life
  from someone with the same body and no carer;
- someone who is lied to constantly, or lives under chronic political instability,
  thinks and feels worse, through no fault of their own;
- someone suffering a heat wave cannot stop it, but has indirect, delayed influence on
  future ones together with others.

Working hypothesis, to be tested, not assumed:

(a) Three things combine into realized functioning:
    - INFLOW: what reaches a node through its relations (care, information, trust, or lies);
    - STOCK: the commons, shared conditions nobody owns or controls alone (climate,
      information ecosystem, political stability, institutions, care capacity);
    - OUTFLOW: what the node gives back to its relations and to the commons.
    Realized functioning ≈ own capacity, transformed by inflow, within the commons.
    Outflow renews or depletes the commons, which sets the next inflow: a reciprocal loop.

(b) The same dimensions that describe a person's or AI's health of intelligence also
    describe the commons: is the information ecosystem truthful (grounding)? Can society
    correct its errors (correction)? Do institutions keep lessons (learning/retention)?
    Are climate, energy and care capacity sustained (resource viability)? Does it absorb
    shocks (robustness)? One profile, read at three levels: node, relation, commons.

(c) Agency has REACH that changes by level: direct and fast (own body, choices);
    relational (being someone's carer, or someone's liar); commons-level (slow, diffuse,
    only together with others). The further out the reach, the more delayed and shared
    the return. EbE's commons proofs show that steep discounting and many takers make
    depleting the commons pay. A measure that makes this level visible may counter that.

(d) Relations are two-sided. A carer who burns out is commons depletion, not support.

EbE has two related formal results (Lean, NetworkVortexLedger.lean):
- internallyViable_iff_exists_viable_transfer: "A nonempty whole is viable exactly when
  some pure transfer lets every part cover its own maintenance."
- transfer_breaks_part_with_whole_unchanged: a transfer can break one part (e.g. the
  commons) while the whole's total slack is unchanged; averages hide who is depleted.

3. Questions

Q1. Prior art. Has anyone measured individuals and their shared conditions with the SAME
    dimensions, and linked them through inflow and outflow? What is closest?
    Separate: what exists; what is a partial synthesis; what was not found in your search.

Q2. Map the literature. At minimum check these families. The names are leads to verify,
    not established facts:
    - Capability approach, especially conversion factors (personal, social, environmental):
      Sen, Robeyns, Nussbaum; ICECAP.
    - WHO ICF environmental factors (facilitators/barriers); capacity vs performance.
    - Social determinants of health (Marmot; WHO Commission 2008); social gradient.
    - Carer and family spillover in health economics and QALY studies; carer burden instruments.
    - Ethics of care and relational autonomy (e.g. Kittay, Tronto, Mackenzie & Stoljar).
    - Social capital and collective efficacy (Putnam, Coleman, Sampson; Bandura).
    - Wellbeing accounting that separates current wellbeing from capital stocks for future
      wellbeing (OECD How's Life; Stiglitz–Sen–Fitoussi; inclusive wealth; Dasgupta review).
    - Planetary health; Raworth's doughnut (social foundation, ecological ceiling).
    - Ostrom and knowledge commons (Hess & Ostrom); epistemic health of communities
      (Piovarchy & Siskind 2023); information ecosystem quality; democratic quality indices
      (e.g. V-Dem); social trust.
    - Empirical links from shared conditions to cognition and mental health: heat and
      cognition, chronic stress, insecurity, misinformation exposure, loneliness.
    - Network and contagion studies of health and behaviour, including their critiques.
    - Risk-sharing and mutual-insurance networks; resilience of social-ecological systems.
    - AI side: the AI's own commons (training data, the web as a knowledge source, model
      collapse and its counterevidence, human verification labour, compute and energy),
      and what "inflow", "outflow" and "reach" mean for an AI system.
    For each: main object, level (node/relation/commons), what it measures, and which of
    the five dimensions plus inflow/stock/outflow/reach it covers (a coverage table).

Q3. Test the hypothesis. Is the inflow/stock/outflow framing supported, redundant with an
    existing framework, or misleading? Is "one profile at three levels" defensible, or do
    commons dimensions need different constructs? Is "reach" already defined elsewhere
    (e.g. sphere of influence, political efficacy, locus of control)? How should the
    combination of capacity, inflow and commons be modelled (profile, interaction,
    threshold, product)? Apply the same caution as the earlier reports about products.

Q4. Formal link. Do the two Lean results above correspond to known formal models
    (risk-sharing, transfers in networks, viability theory, kin or reciprocal support)?
    What would a formal statement of "the carer who burns out" look like?

Q5. A candidate measurement design, unvalidated. For persons, AI systems and
    institutions:
    - the three layers of a node's profile (own capacity; supported; commons-conditioned);
    - an inflow profile (what the network provides, and how fragile it is);
    - a reach profile (what the node gives back, at which level);
    - a commons profile on the same dimensions.
    Name indicators and existing data sources where possible (surveys, indices, registries).
    Define the unit of analysis and accounting boundary first.

Q6. Equity and misuse. Address at least: ranking families or communities; blaming
    carers; making unpaid care invisible or demanding more of it; a person's claim to care
    never depending on their outflow; who decides what a healthy commons is; political
    misuse; Goodhart effects.

Q7. What this teaches EbE. What is strengthened, challenged, or missing in the meta-model?

Q8. Falsifiable predictions. Three to six, each with a design, an outcome, and what would
    count against it.
